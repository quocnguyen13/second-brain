## Summary
Eligible complainants are consumers, micro-enterprises, small businesses, smaller charities and trusts, CBTL consumers and guarantors [S1]. A micro-enterprise has fewer than 10 staff and turnover or balance sheet of €2 million at most [S2]; a small business has turnover under £6.5 million and fewer than 50 staff or a balance sheet under £5 million [S3]. The bank has eight weeks to send a final response, or 15 business days for payment and e-money complaints [S4]. The complainant must go to the bank first; the Ombudsman is free [S5]. The Ombudsman can act once a final response is sent or eight weeks have passed, and not more than six months after the final response, or more than six years after the event (three years from awareness, if later), unless circumstances were exceptional or the bank consents [S6]. For complaints referred from 1 April 2026 the cap is £455,000 for acts from 1 April 2019 and £205,000 before, indexed to CPI each year; interest and costs sit outside it [S7, S8]. The Ombudsman decides on what is fair and reasonable; a decision binds the bank only if the complainant accepts it [S9].

## Facts
### Complaining to the bank first
- For most complaints, the firm must send a final response, or a written response explaining why it cannot yet, within eight weeks of receiving the complaint. · [S4] "by the end of eight weeks after its receipt of the complaint, send the complainant:" · DISP 1.6.2R · rule dated 2019-07-12
- For a payment services (PSD) or e-money (EMD) complaint, the deadline is 15 business days, extendable to 35 business days in exceptional circumstances. · [S4] "send a final response to the complainant by the end of 15 business days" · DISP 1.6.2AR · rule dated 2018-01-13
- A final response must tell the complainant they can refer to the Ombudsman, enclose its standard leaflet, and say whether the firm consents to waive the time limits. · [S4] "informs the complainant that if he remains dissatisfied with the respondent's response," · DISP 1.6.2R(1)(d)–(f) · rule dated 2019-07-12
- The Ombudsman tells consumers to complain to the business first so it can put things right. · [S5] "The financial product or service provider should have the chance to sort things out." · Step 1 · undated
- The Ombudsman's service is free for the complainant. · [S5] "Our service is free and easy to use." · Step 3 · undated
- The Ombudsman can consider a complaint once the firm has sent its final response, or once eight weeks have passed since the firm received it. · [S6] "eight weeks have elapsed since the respondent received the complaint" · DISP 2.8.1R(1)–(2) · rule dated 2018-01-13

### Who can complain
- An eligible complainant is a consumer, a micro-enterprise, a charity with annual income under £6.5 million, a trustee of a trust with net assets under £5 million, a CBTL consumer, a small business, or a guarantor. · [S1] "a charity which has an annual income of less than £6.5 million" · DISP 2.7.3R · rule dated 2019-04-01
- The complaint must also arise from one of the relationships with the firm that the rules list. · [S1] "must also have a complaint which arises from matters relevant to one or more of the following relationships" · DISP 2.7.6R · rule dated 2024-11-30
- A micro-enterprise employs fewer than 10 persons and has a turnover or annual balance sheet of no more than €2 million. · [S2] "(a) employs fewer than 10 persons; and (b) has a turnover or annual balance sheet that does not exceed €2 million" · Glossary, micro-enterprise · undated
- For DISP, a small business is not a micro-enterprise, has annual turnover under £6.5 million, and either employs fewer than 50 persons or has a balance sheet under £5 million. · [S3] "has an annual turnover of less than £6.5 million … employs fewer than 50 persons; or … balance sheet total of less than £5 million" · Glossary, small business, item (2) · undated
- Partner and linked enterprises count in the size tests: if the parent fails them, so does the complainant. · [S1] "then neither will the complainant" · DISP 2.7.4G · dated 2019-04-01
- A firm in doubt about a business, charity or trust's eligibility should treat it as eligible. · [S1] "it should treat the complainant as if it were eligible" · DISP 2.7.5G · dated 2008-04-06

### Time limits
- The Ombudsman cannot consider a complaint referred more than six months after the firm sent its final response. · [S6] "more than six months after the date on which the respondent sent the complainant its final response" · DISP 2.8.2R(1) · rule dated 2016-06-30
- The Ombudsman states the same six-month limit to consumers. · [S5] "You will need to make a complaint to us within 6 months from the date on your final response." · Step 2 · undated
- It also cannot consider a complaint referred more than six years after the event, or, if later, three years after the complainant became aware of cause for complaint. · [S6] "six years after the event complained of" … "three years from the date on which the complainant became aware" · DISP 2.8.2R(2) · rule dated 2016-06-30
- The limits do not apply where missing them was due to exceptional circumstances, or where the firm consents. · [S6] "the respondent has consented to the Ombudsman considering the complaint" · DISP 2.8.2R(3), (5) · rule dated 2016-06-30
- Incapacity of the complainant is given as an example of exceptional circumstances. · [S6] "An example of exceptional circumstances might be where the complainant has been or is incapacitated." · DISP 2.8.4G · dated 2008-04-06

### The most it can award
- The rule sets base caps of £350,000 for acts or omissions on or after 1 April 2019 and £160,000 for earlier ones. · [S7] "£350,000 for a complaint concerning an act or omission which occurred on or after 1 April 2019" · DISP 3.7.4 · rule dated 2026-03-26
- The caps rise on 1 April each year by CPI since January 2019, rounded down to the nearest £5,000, for complaints referred on or after that date. · [S7] "applying the percentage increase in CPI between January 2019 and January of that year" · DISP 3.7.4(2) · rule dated 2026-03-26
- For complaints referred on or after 1 April 2026, the caps are £455,000 (acts from 1 April 2019) and £205,000 (earlier acts). · [S7] table under DISP 3.7.4; [S8] "£455,000 for complaints about acts or omissions by firms on or after 1 April 2019" · in force 2026-04-01
- The 2026 rise was £10,000 and £5,000 on the previous year. · [S8] "(an increase of £10,000 on the previous year)" · news, published 2026-04-01
- Interest on the award, costs, and interest on costs are excluded from the cap. · [S7] "For the purpose of calculating the maximum money award, the following are excluded:" · DISP 3.7.5G · dated 2008-04-06
- Where fair compensation exceeds the cap, the Ombudsman may recommend the firm pays the balance. · [S7] "he may recommend that the respondent pays the complainant the balance." · DISP 3.7.6G · dated 2015-07-31

### How the decision binds
- The Ombudsman decides by what is, in its opinion, fair and reasonable in all the circumstances. · [S9] "fair and reasonable in all the circumstances of the case." · FSMA 2000 s. 228(2) · revised text up to date 2026-10-12
- A determination binds the firm and the complainant only if the complainant accepts it; silence by the deadline counts as rejection. · [S9] "If the complainant notifies the ombudsman that he accepts the determination, it is binding on the respondent" · s. 228(5)–(6)
- The firm must comply promptly, and a registered money award can be enforced through the courts. · [S7] "a complainant can enforce through the courts a money award registered by the Ombudsman" · DISP 3.7.12R, 3.7.13G · dated 2008-04-06
