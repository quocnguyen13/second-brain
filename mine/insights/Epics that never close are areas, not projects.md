# Epics that never close are areas, not projects

A backlog full of standing epics, such as "Onboarding improvements" or "Card fraud", is Forte's list of areas under another name. It can't show how much work is committed, and it never shows anything finished. Breaking each standing epic into dated outcomes that close would make the load visible to stakeholders and give the team a steady run of finished work.

## Why I think this
- Forte: seen through areas, "you'll never quite know just how much is on your plate"; "Hiring" could be "anything from a part-time hire every 6 months to filling 50 positions this quarter" ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]).
- An area "continues indefinitely", and Forte: "I couldn't design a better way to kill your motivation if I tried." ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]])
- Breaking responsibilities into projects keeps the list "constantly turning over", which "creates a cadence of regular victories" ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]).
- (reasoning) A standing epic behaves like an area: it has no end state, so its size and progress can't be read from the backlog.
- (reasoning) The mapping to epics is an extension to product work. Forte writes about personal information, not delivery tooling.
