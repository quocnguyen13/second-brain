# An ombudsman decision binds the bank only when the complainant accepts it, so complaint journeys should plan for both outcomes

The ombudsman decides by what is "fair and reasonable in all the circumstances of the case", and the decision becomes "binding on the respondent and the complainant and final" only if the complainant accepts it ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=601]], s. 228(2), (5)). The bank gets no such choice. So a bank's complaint process should treat an upheld decision as something it will have to carry out, and a rejected one as a complaint that may still be open (reasoning).

## Why I think this
- Silence by the deadline counts as rejection, but a late acceptance can still count if the complainant hasn't rejected the decision and the scheme operator's conditions are met ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=601]], s. 228(6)–(6A)). The case doesn't close on the deadline date (reasoning).
- Once accepted, a money award can be enforced by the complainant, and a direction by injunction ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=603]], s. 229(8)–(9)). A direction can require steps "whether or not a court could order those steps" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=602]], s. 229(2)(b)). Redress may therefore mean a change to a product or a process, not only a payment (reasoning).
- Decisions are published, without the complainant's name unless they agree ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=605]], s. 230A(1)–(3)). A pattern of upheld complaints about one product becomes visible outside the bank (reasoning).
- The ombudsman can require the documents it considers necessary, and a failure without reasonable excuse can be dealt with as contempt ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=606]], ss. 231(1)–(3), 232(1); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=607]], s. 232(2)). Product records need to be retrievable for each complaint (reasoning).
- What a complainant gives up by rejecting a decision isn't in `raw/` (reasoning).
