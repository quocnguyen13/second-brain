# FCA-PRA coordination is mutual by default, but stability outranks conduct through a one-way PRA veto

Most of the coordination machinery is symmetric: consult, share information, keep a joint memorandum. The exception shows the hierarchy. Where the PRA thinks an FCA action on a PRA-authorised firm could threaten stability, it can stop that action. The FCA has no matching power over the PRA in Part 1A. For a dual-regulated bank, this means a conduct remedy the FCA wants can be held back on prudential grounds, never the other way round.

## Why I think this
- The coordination duty is mutual. Each regulator consults the other before action with a "material adverse effect" on the other's objectives, and seeks the other's information and advice ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3D(1)).
- The memorandum of understanding is joint, reviewed each year, and published ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3E).
- Only the PRA can direct the other regulator "not to exercise the power", and only on stability, failure or core-services grounds ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3I(4)–(6)).
- The FCA can refuse only where compliance would breach an international obligation of the UK ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3I(8)).
- Consolidated-supervision directions can run either way, but FCA directions under s. 3M are "subject to" PRA directions under ss. 3I–3J ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3M(11)).
- The veto is transparent: reasons in writing, publication unless that is against the public interest, and copies to the Treasury and Parliament ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3L). That limits it as a routine tool (reasoning).
- "Never the other way round" holds within Part 1A. Other parts of FSMA could contain FCA powers this clip doesn't show (reasoning).
