# For a lender, a permission gap puts the loan book at risk, not just a compliance finding

For most regulated activities, an authorised firm that goes beyond its permission faces a regulatory breach, and its contracts stand. Credit is the exception: lending, administering or collecting outside permission is a crime, and the loans can't be enforced against borrowers until the FCA says so. So checking permissions for a new lending product belongs in product design, on the critical path, not in a sign-off at the end (reasoning).

## Why I think this
- Outside credit, acting beyond permission is treated as breaching a requirement imposed by the FCA (and the PRA, for a PRA-authorised person). It is not an offence and doesn't make any transaction void or unenforceable ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=52]], s. 20(1)–(2)).
- For a "credit-related regulated activity", acting outside permission is an offence, punishable by up to two years' imprisonment on indictment ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=60]], s. 23(1A), (1F)). Credit-related activities include entering into or administering a credit agreement and collecting debts under one, but not agreements secured on land ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=60]], s. 23(1C)–(1D)).
- Agreements made that way are "unenforceable against the other party", who can recover what they paid plus compensation ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=63]], s. 26A(1)–(2)). The book can be rescued only if the FCA, on the firm's application, finds it "just and equitable", weighing whether the firm reasonably believed it wasn't breaching s. 20 ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=67]], s. 28A(3)–(5)).
- The same follows for a whole portfolio: an application may cover agreements "of a specified description or made at a specified time" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=67]], s. 28A(7)). A gap that lasts across a product's life could taint every loan written during it (reasoning).
- Servicing and collections need permission of their own: whoever holds the lender's rights can enforce only with permission, or as an appointed representative or exempt person, for administering and for collecting ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=64]], s. 26A(4)–(5)). A change of servicer or collections partner is a permission question as well as an outsourcing one (reasoning).
- What this leaves open: which activities the Treasury has designated as credit-related is in an order that isn't in `raw/` ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=60]], s. 23(1B)).
