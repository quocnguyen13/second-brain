# A product that needs a new regulated activity should be planned around a six-month clock that starts only when the application is complete

A firm has one permission and changes it by applying for a variation ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=83]], s. 55A(3)). For a bank, the PRA decides, with the FCA's consent ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=89]], s. 55I(1)). The regulator has up to 6 months from the completed application, and the consent falls inside the same period ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=110]], s. 55V(1), (3)). So the date the roadmap controls is the date the application is complete, not the date it is sent (reasoning).

## Why I think this
- The 6 months run from receipt of "the completed application"; an incomplete one may take up to 12 months from receipt ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=110]], s. 55V(1)–(2)). A thin application can double the outer limit (reasoning).
- Until it decides, the regulator may ask for further information, and for an application to the PRA either regulator may ask ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=108]], s. 55U(5)–(6)). Each request is time the product team doesn't control (reasoning).
- The permission may come back narrower than asked, with limitations or requirements attached ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=86]], s. 55E(4)–(5); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=87]], s. 55F(3)–(4); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=97]], s. 55L(1)). The product design should be described in the terms the permission will use, so the grant matches what will be built (reasoning).
- Launching before the grant breaches a requirement ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=52]], s. 20(1)); the clock is a hard dependency, not a soft one (reasoning).
- The Act sets the maximum only. How long decisions usually take is not in `raw/` (reasoning).
