# An outsourcer's readiness letter is a forecast, not evidence, so go-live needs proof of what the supplier has already done

A bank stays fully responsible for what it outsources, including what its supplier sub-contracts. A letter in which the supplier says it expects to be ready is not assurance. Before go-live, a product owner relying on an outsourcer should ask for statements of completed readiness work, refreshed close to the decision, and for its critical sub-suppliers too, even inside the same group.

## Why I think this
- A firm that outsources critical or important functions "remains fully responsible" for its regulatory obligations, and must make sure the provider has the ability and capacity to perform reliably (SYSC 8.1.6R, 8.1.8R, [[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=106]]).
- TSB's supplier, SABIS, was a company in TSB's own group, and relied on 85 fourth parties with whom TSB had no contract. TSB kept its regulatory responsibility for them ([[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=76]], para 4.247).
- SABIS's readiness letter of 5 April 2018 and its suppliers' confirmations were "forward looking statements of good intention or expectations rather than statements of fact", all but one caveated, and TSB didn't ask for fresh ones before the 18 April decision ([[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=78]], para 4.255; [[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=79]], para 4.258).
- The IT function's attestation on SABIS repeated SABIS's expectation and attested to no steps SABIS had taken ([[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=80]], para 4.261).
- The FCA counted the lack of assurance "in the form of statements of proven fact about the completeness of readiness activities already undertaken" in TSB's Principle 2 breach ([[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=94]], para 5.3(b)(v)).
- So the test of a supplier's readiness statement is tense: "we have done" is evidence, "we will be" is not (reasoning). The same holds when the supplier is a group company: being in the same group lets a firm take its control of the provider into account (SYSC 8.1.10R, [[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=108]]), but at TSB that did not replace a formal assessment (reasoning).
