# FCA authorisation is a gate, not a licence, so a regulated product carries supervision cost for its whole life

The FCA describes its work in three stages: authorise, supervise, enforce. Only the first happens before launch. The other two run for as long as the firm carries on the activity, and they are sharper where the consumer impact is higher. For a lending, cards or payments roadmap, the regulatory effort should be planned as a running cost tied to each product's risk profile, not as a one-off launch milestone.

## Why I think this
- A firm must show it meets "a range of requirements" before authorisation. The FCA then supervises it "to make sure it continues to meet our standards and rules once it's authorised" ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- Supervision is proportionate: the FCA gives priority to firms and areas posing higher risk to its objectives, weighing "size, complexity and potential impact on different types of consumers" ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- It uses data to "move faster" against firms and individuals more likely to cause harm. Its enforcement powers reach criminal prosecution ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- The duty is statutory and covers every authorised person, dual-regulated banks included ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1L(1)).
- So the regulatory cost of a product doesn't stop at launch, and it rises with the product's consumer impact (reasoning). How much supervision a given product actually draws isn't in these sources; it would take the FCA's approach to supervision (reasoning).
