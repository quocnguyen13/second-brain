# Small-business complaints should be handled as Ombudsman-eligible by default, because the rules put the burden of doubt on the bank

Testing a business customer's eligibility before handling its complaint saves little and risks much. The tests depend on data the bank often doesn't hold: staff numbers, turnover, balance sheet and the wider group. A bank in doubt must treat the business as eligible, and the Ombudsman decides in the end. A business-banking complaint journey should therefore send every final response with the FOS information. It should record eligibility evidence only when it is already to hand.

## Why I think this
- Micro-enterprises and small businesses are eligible complainants ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]], Eligible complainants).
- A micro-enterprise has fewer than 10 staff and turnover or a balance sheet of €2 million or less ([[raw/fca-glossary-micro-enterprise.md]]). A small business has turnover under £6.5 million and either fewer than 50 staff or a balance sheet under £5 million ([[raw/fca-glossary-small-business.md]], (2)).
- Partner and linked enterprises count: a subsidiary of a majority parent that fails the tests fails them too ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]).
- "If a respondent is in doubt about the eligibility of a business, charity or trust, it should treat the complainant as if it were eligible". The Ombudsman determines eligibility from evidence "such as audited accounts or VAT returns" ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]).
- A final response must enclose the FOS leaflet, give the FOS website and say whether the bank waives the time limits ([[raw/fca-disp-1-6-complaints-time-limits.md]], DISP 1.6.2R(1)).
- The group and balance-sheet tests need data outside a typical account record, so an upfront test would often end in doubt anyway, and doubt means eligible (reasoning).
- The trade-off: the default sends some ineligible businesses to the FOS. But the Ombudsman, not the bank, makes the final call, and in doubt the bank has to treat the business as eligible anyway (reasoning).
