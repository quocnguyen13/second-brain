# In a dual-regulated bank every product answers to both regulators, even products with no PRA-regulated activity

Dual regulation attaches to the firm, not the product. Once one PRA-regulated activity is in the firm's permission, the whole firm sits in the PRA's population. So a payments or cards product built inside such a bank can meet prudential constraints and PRA–FCA coordination that the same product would not meet in an FCA-only payments firm. For product owners, "which regulator?" is a question about the legal entity the product launches from, not about the product.

## Why I think this
- A "PRA-authorised person" is an authorised person whose permission includes "one or more PRA-regulated activities" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2B(5)). The test is on the whole permission.
- The PRA must supervise PRA-authorised persons ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2K). The FCA must supervise all authorised persons ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1L(1)).
- The PRA's general objective is aimed at the firm's safety and soundness, not at particular products ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2B(2)–(3)).
- The PRA can stop FCA action against "a particular PRA-authorised person" on stability grounds, whatever product line the action concerns ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3I(2)(a)).
- So the choice of launch entity is a regulatory design decision. The same payments or cards feature carries different oversight depending on whether it launches inside a PRA-authorised entity or in a separate entity with no PRA-regulated activity (reasoning).
- Whether that difference is material depends on which activities s. 22A lists, and the source doesn't include it (reasoning).
