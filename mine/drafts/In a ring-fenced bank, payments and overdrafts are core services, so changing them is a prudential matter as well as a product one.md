# In a ring-fenced bank, payments and overdrafts are core services, so changing them is a prudential matter as well as a product one

The PRA names retail deposits and their related payment and overdraft services as the core services that ring-fencing protects. In a ring-fenced bank, the current-account bundle is therefore part of the PRA's general objective, not just a conduct matter for the FCA. Changes to how those services run, where they sit in the group or what they depend on should go through prudential as well as conduct review.

## Why I think this
- The PRA's general objective includes ring-fencing "to support the safeguarding of the continuity of core services (retail deposits and related payment and overdraft services)" ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=8]]).
- The PRA must seek to protect RFBs from risks "arising in the UK or elsewhere" that could disrupt core services, and to minimise the risk that a group member's failure affects them ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=8]]; [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=3]]).
- RFBs are supervised against more requirements than other banks, and the PRA has group restructuring powers to enforce ring-fencing ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=54]]).
- In resolution, continuity of critical functions, including core services, is what an orderly failure must keep ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=12]]).
- So a change that moves a payment or overdraft dependency outside the RFB, for example onto a group-wide platform, touches what the PRA is told to protect (reasoning). The sources don't say which changes need notification or approval, so that is the next thing to check (reasoning).
