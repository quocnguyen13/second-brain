# Memex

A hypothetical desk-based personal device, proposed by [[wiki/entities/Vannevar Bush]] in 1945, that stores a lifetime of books, records, and correspondence on microfilm and lets the user link any two items into a reviewable, shareable "trail" ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=15]], [[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=16]], [[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=17]]).

## What the sources say
- Physically a desk with slanting translucent viewing screens, a keyboard, and buttons and levers; bulk storage on microfilm, with a transparent platen for photographing new pages directly into the record ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=15]]).
- At the scale Bush estimated, inserting 5,000 pages a day would still take hundreds of years to fill the device, so the owner "can be profligate and enter material freely" ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=15]]).
- Supports conventional keyword lookup (tapping a code to jump straight to a book) ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=15]]) but its defining feature is [[wiki/concepts/Associative indexing]] ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=16]]).
- Illustrated with a worked example: a user researching why the Turkish bow beat the English longbow in the Crusades builds a trail across an encyclopedia, a history book, and elasticity tables, adds his own notes, and years later replays and shares the trail with a friend, who splices it into his own memex ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=16]], [[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=17]]).
- Bush predicted "wholly new forms of encyclopedias," pre-built with associative trails running through them, ready to drop into a memex ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=17]]).
- [[wiki/sources/Source - LLM Wiki]] frames the compiled wiki this vault implements as related in spirit to the memex — calling Bush's vision private and actively curated — and says the part Bush couldn't solve, who does the maintenance, is what an LLM now handles ([[raw/karpathy-llm-wiki.md]]).
