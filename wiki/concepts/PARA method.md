# PARA method

A system that files all digital information into four top-level categories: Projects, Areas, Resources and Archives ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]).

## What the sources say
- **Projects:** short-term efforts, at work or at home, taken on with a goal in mind. Examples: "Buy a new computer", "Write research report" ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]).
- **Areas** of responsibility: parts of work and life that need ongoing attention. Examples: Product Management, Direct Reports, Health, Finances ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]).
- **Resources:** topics you are interested in or learning about. Examples: graphic design, habit formation, notetaking ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]).
- **Archives:** anything from the other three that is no longer active but worth keeping. That means finished or paused projects, inactive areas, and resources you have lost interest in ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]).
- The structure is four top-level folders, each with a small number of subfolders. It is meant to work across platforms: file system, cloud storage or a notes app ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]).
- Simplicity is the point. A system "as complex as your life" costs the time and energy it should free ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]).
- **Projects vs. areas** is the distinction the post argues hardest:
  - A project ends; an area continues indefinitely ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]).
  - A list of areas hides your real workload. "Hiring" could mean one part-time hire or fifty positions ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]).
  - A list of areas never turns over, so it gives no sense of progress. Breaking areas into projects produces "a cadence of regular victories" ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]).
- The organising principle behind the categories is [[wiki/concepts/Organizing by actionability]] ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]).

## Where sources disagree
- **A hierarchy with one home per item, or associative links.**
  - Forte: four top-level folders with subfolders, one home per item, so "you'll know exactly where to put everything, and exactly where to find it" ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]).
  - Matuschak: "prefer associative ontologies to hierarchical taxonomies" ([[raw/matuschak-evergreen-notes.md]]).
  - Bush: filing an item in one place under one classification path is artificial, because the mind works by association ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=14]]).
  - Scope: Forte is filing working material for action; Matuschak and Bush are linking ideas and records for thinking and recall. The captured Matuschak page gives the principle's title only, not the reasons behind it.
