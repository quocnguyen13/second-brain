# Eligible complainant

An eligible complainant is someone who may take a complaint to the Financial Ombudsman Service. They must be in an eligible class, such as a consumer or a small business, and the complaint must arise from a listed relationship with the firm ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]).

## What the sources say
- **The classes** ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]], Eligible complainants):
  - consumer;
  - micro-enterprise;
  - charity with annual income under £6.5 million;
  - trustee of a trust with net assets under £5 million;
  - CBTL consumer;
  - small business;
  - guarantor.
- **When the business test applies** ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]):
  - **Most classes.** The test applies when the complainant complains to the firm.
  - **A micro-enterprise with a payment-services complaint.** It is eligible if it was a micro-enterprise either when the payment-service contract was made or when it complained.

### Business customers (emphasis)
- **The two business tiers:**

  | Tier | Test | Source |
  |---|---|---|
  | Micro-enterprise | fewer than 10 staff **and** turnover or annual balance sheet not over €2 million | ([[raw/fca-glossary-micro-enterprise.md]]) |
  | Small business (DISP meaning) | not a micro-enterprise; turnover under £6.5 million; **and** either fewer than 50 staff **or** balance sheet total under £5 million | ([[raw/fca-glossary-small-business.md]], (2)) |

  Both tiers apply articles 3 to 6 of the Annex to the Micro-enterprise Recommendation ([[raw/fca-glossary-micro-enterprise.md]]; [[raw/fca-glossary-small-business.md]]). The Glossary's other "small business" meaning, turnover under £1 million, is for COMP, not complaints ([[raw/fca-glossary-small-business.md]], (1)).
- **Above both tiers.** The list of eligible classes is closed: an eligible complainant "must be" one of them. A larger business is therefore eligible only if it fits another class, such as a charity or trust under their own limits, or a guarantor ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]], Eligible complainants).
- **Groups.** Partner and linked enterprises count, as defined in the Micro-enterprise Recommendation. A subsidiary whose majority-owning parent fails the tests fails them too ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]).
- **Benefit of the doubt.** A firm unsure whether a business, charity or trust is eligible should treat it as eligible. If the complaint reaches the FOS, the Ombudsman decides on evidence such as audited accounts or VAT returns ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]).
- **Professional clients excluded.** In the Compulsory Jurisdiction, a complainant who was a professional client or eligible counterparty for the activity in question is not eligible, unless it is a consumer for that activity ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]], DISP 2.7.9R).

### The relationship test
- **The rule.** The complaint must arise from a relationship listed in DISP 2.7.6R ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]).
- **For a bank, the main relationships are:**
  - customer, payment service user or e-money holder, actual or potential;
  - payer, where the bank is the payee's payment service provider;
  - APP fraud victims, where the bank was involved in the transfer and the complaint is not a PSD complaint. This covers a complaint that the bank didn't do enough to prevent or respond to the fraud;
  - guarantor;
  - recipient of a banker's reference;
  - a person the bank has pursued for payment under a credit agreement.
- **PEPs.** Someone the firm has identified as a politically exposed person, or as a family member or known close associate of one, can complain about that identification and what followed from it ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]).
