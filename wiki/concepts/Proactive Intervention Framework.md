# Proactive Intervention Framework

The PRA's five-stage scale of how close a firm is to failure, used to identify risks to a firm's viability early and to set the supervisory response ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=44]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=45]]).

## What the sources say
- **What the score measures.** Each firm has a PIF score reflecting the PRA's judgement of its proximity to failure. Supervisors use it when drawing up the firm's supervisory plan and update it regularly ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=44]]).
- **What feeds it.** External context, business risk, management and governance, risk management and controls, capital, liquidity and operational resilience. It is **not** sensitive to a firm's potential impact or its resolvability ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=44]]). Potential impact is a separate scale: see [[wiki/concepts/Potential impact categories]].
- **Not disclosed.** The PRA does not routinely tell the market or the firm itself its PIF judgement, because disclosure could destabilise a firm in stress ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=45]]).
- **Moving up a stage.** When a firm moves to a higher stage, supervisors deploy more supervisory actions, senior management must take remedial action, and the authorities prepare for resolution in parallel ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=45]]).
- **The five stages** ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=45]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=46]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=47]]):

| Stage | Meaning | Possible actions |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Low risk to viability | Normal supervision | Normal risk assessment, including recovery and resolution planning |
| 2 · Moderate risk to viability | Vulnerabilities found in the financial position or in risk management or governance | More intense supervision; extra reporting; information-gathering and investigatory powers; firm must fix deficiencies in a set period and update, possibly activate, its recovery plan. Resolution: the Bank may intensify contingency planning; the FSCS may check the quality of Single Customer View data |
| 3 · Risk to viability absent action by the firm | Significant threats to safety and soundness | The PRA may require changes to management or the board, limits on distributions (dividends, variable pay), restrictions on activities, a limit on balance-sheet growth or tighter leverage limits, tighter liquidity or capital requirements; firm may have to use its recovery options |
| 4 · Imminent risk to viability | A real risk the firm will fail the [[wiki/concepts/Threshold Conditions]], some chance of correction remains | Larger recovery actions on a set timetable; recovery actions, including asset disposal or sale of the firm, must be completed; the Bank and FSCS confirm resolution preparation, including data |
| 5 · In resolution or being wound up | — | The Bank uses its resolution powers; the FSCS may pay out depositors or fund a transfer |

- **Shared with the FCA.** The PRA routinely tells the FCA the PIF position of every dual-regulated firm or firm in a dual-regulated group ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=7]]).
- **Use of powers.** A firm's PIF position is one factor in whether the PRA uses formal powers ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=51]]).
