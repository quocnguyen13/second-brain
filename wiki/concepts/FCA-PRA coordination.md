# FCA-PRA coordination

These are the statutory mechanisms that make the FCA and the PRA act as a system: a duty to coordinate, a memorandum of understanding, directions between the regulators, and Treasury boundary orders ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 3D–3R).

## What the sources say

### 1. The duty to coordinate (s. 3D)
- **What the regulators must do.** They must coordinate so that each ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3D(1)):
  - consults the other before any action that may have a "material adverse effect" on the other's objectives
  - gets information and advice from the other on matters of common regulatory interest
  - applies efficiency and proportionality (principles 3B(1)(a) and (b)) on shared matters
- **Limits.** The duty applies only as far as it is compatible with each regulator's objectives and not disproportionately burdensome ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3D(2)).
- **"Common regulatory interest".** A function relates to a matter of common regulatory interest when ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3D(3)):
  - the other regulator has similar functions over the same persons
  - it covers similar subject matter for different persons
  - using it could affect the other regulator's objectives
- **Which objectives count.** For functions that are not general functions, the secondary objectives don't count toward "material adverse effect" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3D(5)).

### 2. The memorandum of understanding (ss. 3E–3F)
- **What it must describe.** Each regulator's role on matters of common interest, and how the regulators will comply with s. 3D ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3E(1)).
- **What it may cover** ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3E(2)):
  - Part 4A permissions and variations
  - requirements
  - information
  - groups mixing PRA-authorised and other authorised persons
  - rules
  - rule waivers
  - investigations
  - change in control
  - Lloyd's
  - the register
  - insolvency
  - fees
- **What it must cover.** Relations with overseas regulators and the compensation scheme ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3E(3)).
- **Upkeep.** The regulators must review it at least once each calendar year, send it to the Treasury (which lays it before Parliament), and publish it. They may leave out parts that are against the public interest or purely technical ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3E(4)–(8)).
- **With-profits.** A separate or combined memorandum covers with-profits insurers ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3F).

### 3. The PRA's veto over the FCA (ss. 3I–3L)
- **Three conditions** ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3I(2)–(5)):
  1. The FCA proposes to use regulatory or insolvency powers over PRA-authorised persons, generally, by class or individually.
  2. The PRA thinks that use may threaten UK financial stability, cause a failure of a PRA-authorised person that harms the UK financial system, or threaten the continuity of core services.
  3. The PRA thinks a direction is necessary to avoid that.
- **Effect.** The direction stops the FCA using the power, or using it in a specified way, for a period or until revoked. The FCA may decline only where compliance would breach an international obligation of the UK ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3I(6)–(8)).
- **With-profits.** A parallel power covers FCA action affecting discretionary benefits under with-profits policies, where the direction is "desirable" for the PRA's objectives ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3J).
- **Procedure.** The PRA must consult the FCA first and give written reasons. It must publish the direction and give a copy to the firm concerned, unless that is against the public interest, a decision it must keep under review. It copies s. 3I directions to the Treasury, which lays them before Parliament ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3L). The PRA can revoke a direction at any time ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3K).
- **One way only.** Part 1A gives the FCA no matching power to restrain the PRA. Directions under s. 3M run in either direction, but only for consolidated supervision ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 3I, 3M).

### 4. Consolidated supervision of groups (ss. 3M–3P)
- **Who can direct whom.** Where only one regulator is the competent authority for consolidated supervision of a group, it may direct the other to exercise, or not exercise, a relevant function over authorised members of the group ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3M(1), (4)–(5)).
- **Excluded functions.** Rule-making, codes, general policy and guidance are excluded ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3M(7)).
- **Obligations.** The directed regulator must comply as soon as practicable. FCA directions under s. 3M are subject to PRA directions under ss. 3I–3J ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3M(9)–(11)).
- **Procedure.** The directing regulator must consult first, give written reasons, and publish. The directed regulator shares consultation responses with it ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 3O–3P).

### 5. The Treasury boundary (ss. 3G–3H)
- **Orders.** The Treasury can allocate matters on PRA-authorised persons to one regulator, tell a regulator what to have regard to, or require consultation ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3G).
- **Parliamentary control.** Orders are affirmative. Urgent orders lapse after 28 days without approval ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3H).

### 6. Other links
- **Guidance.** Each regulator consults the other before issuing guidance on its objectives ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 1K(2), 2I(2)).
- **Public consultations.** Each discloses its engagement with the other regulator's statutory panels ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 1RB, 2NB).
- **Panel membership.** Anyone paid by the FCA, the PRA, the Payment Systems Regulator, the Bank or the Treasury is disqualified from sitting on the statutory panels, unless Treasury regulations say otherwise ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 1MA, 2LA).
- **Services.** The regulators may provide services to each other ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3R(1)).

### 7. The PRA's account of the relationship
- **Separate mandates.** The FCA is the conduct regulator for the firms the PRA regulates prudentially. The key principle is that each regulator focuses on the key risks to its own objectives while staying aware of the other's concerns ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=16]]).
- **Sharing and colleges.** Conclusions materially relevant to the other regulator are exchanged; information on dual-regulated firms is shared; domestic supervisory colleges meet at a frequency reflecting the firm's importance to the other regulator ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=16]]). The PRA uses the FCA's findings on key conduct risks, including money laundering, where they bear on its objectives ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=49]]).
- **Cross-membership.** The PRA CEO sits on the FCA Board and the FCA CEO on the PRC; both CEOs sit on the FPC ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=16]]).
- **Joint gates.** A new deposit-taker needs both regulators satisfied on their Threshold Conditions, with the PRA leading and deciding ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=42]]). A candidate for an SMF the PRA approves needs both satisfied on fitness and propriety ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=23]]).

### 8. Who leads on what: the 2024 MoU
The current memorandum, signed 2024-03-26 under ss. 3D and 3E, replaces the 2019 one ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=1]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=2]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=20]]). For a dual-regulated bank:

| Process | Lead | The other regulator's role |
|---|---|---|
| Permissions: grant, vary by adding an activity, remove | PRA | FCA **consent** (a veto); FCA consulted before cancellation ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=21]]) |
| Senior managers (SMFs designated by PRA rules) | PRA | FCA consent ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=21]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=22]]) |
| Customer-facing SMFs (client assets, AML, compliance) | FCA alone | PRA consulted for a subset ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=22]]) |
| Change in control of the bank | PRA | Consults FCA; FCA may direct an objection on AML/terrorist-financing grounds ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=22]]) |
| Prudential supervision | PRA | Not joint; each assesses against its own objectives ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=6]]) |
| Conduct supervision, listing and securities | FCA | Consults PRA before key listing decisions on dual-regulated firms ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=6]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=11]]) |
| FSCS depositor protection rules | PRA | FCA makes rules for other schemes ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=11]]) |
| Banking and ring-fencing transfer schemes (Part VII) | PRA | FCA reviews consumer notices ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=24]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=25]]) |
| Register of firms and senior managers | FCA | single register for both ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=6]]) |
| Investigations and enforcement | Agreed case by case | early notification; joint or single ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=12]]) |

- **Lead regulator, in general.** The PRA leads for dual-regulated firms and the FCA for solo-regulated firms and designated activities; the lead runs a single administrative process ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=6]]).
- **Routine sharing.** PRA to FCA: recovery-plan conclusions, PIF position, capital and liquidity details. FCA to PRA: key conduct-risk findings and material prudential risks in subsidiaries ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=7]]).
- **Escalation.** Serious policy conflicts go to the FCA CEO and the Deputy Governor for Prudential Regulation, then to the FCA Board and the PRC ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=4]]).
- **The veto in practice.** A s. 3I direction is decided by the PRC after a meeting at CEO and Deputy Governor level ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=17]]). It doesn't reach the FCA's concurrent competition powers ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=16]]).
- **Review.** Co-ordination is reviewed each half-year ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=19]]).
