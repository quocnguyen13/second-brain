# Ring-fencing

The regime that separates core retail banking services, deposits with their related payment and overdraft services, into ring-fenced bodies (RFBs) so that their continuity is protected from risks elsewhere in the group or the financial system ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=8]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=54]]).

## What the sources say
- **Core services named.** The PRA's ring-fencing focus safeguards the continuity of "core services (retail deposits and related payment and overdraft services)" ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=8]]); elsewhere it lists them as "deposits, payments, overdrafts" ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=54]]).
- **In the PRA's objective.** The general objective is advanced partly by protecting the continuity of core services at ring-fenced bodies ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2B(3)). The PRA must seek to ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=8]]):
  - ensure RFBs' business avoids any adverse effect on the continuity of core services in the UK
  - protect RFBs from risks, in the UK or elsewhere, that could affect that continuity
  - minimise the risk that the failure of an RFB, or of a member of its group, affects that continuity
- **Also in the MoU.** The PRA's general objective covers its functions on ring-fencing, ring-fenced bodies, their UK parent companies and applications that would create one ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=3]]). The PRA can direct the FCA not to use a power that would threaten the continuity of core services, from a date to be appointed ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=16]]). The PRA leads ring-fencing transfer schemes and consults the FCA before approving the skilled person; the FCA reviews notices to affected consumers ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=24]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=25]]).
- **Added in 2019.** The PRA's general objective was amended in 2019 to require this ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=54]]).
- **How it works.** It restricts the activities of an RFB and gives it a degree of protection from shocks from the rest of its group, the global financial system or the RFB itself. It also supports orderly resolution and continuity of core services after a failure ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=54]]).
- **Supervisory consequences** ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=54]]):
  - RFBs are supervised for compliance with more requirements than other banks
  - the PRA has group restructuring powers over an RFB's group or its unregulated UK holding company
  - the PRA's annual report gives an opinion on RFBs' compliance
  - firms carry primary responsibility for compliance
- **Governance.** In a ring-fenced bank, the board must maintain the RFB's governance independence from the wider group ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=22]]). Critical functions include core services as defined in FSMA Part 9B; banking groups subject to ring-fencing must provide them without interruption through the RFB ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=12]]).
- **If core activities left the PRA.** The FCA would take on a continuity objective for RFBs if a core activity were ever not PRA-regulated ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1IA).
