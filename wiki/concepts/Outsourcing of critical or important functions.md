# Outsourcing of critical or important functions

Relying on a third party for an operational function whose defect or failure "would materially impair" a firm's continuing compliance with its authorisation and regulatory obligations, its financial performance, or the soundness or continuity of its regulated services (SYSC 8.1.4R, as quoted in [[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=105]], Annex A 2.4).

The rules below are as the FCA quoted them for 16 December 2015 to 2018, in its notice to TSB. Current SYSC text is not in `raw/`.

## What the sources say
- **The firm keeps the responsibility.** A firm that outsources critical or important functions "remains fully responsible" for all its regulatory obligations. Senior personnel cannot delegate their responsibility, and the firm's obligations to its clients do not change (SYSC 8.1.6R, [[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=106]]).
- **Avoid undue risk.** When relying on a third party for critical functions, a firm must take reasonable steps to avoid "undue operational risk" (SYSC 8.1.1R, [[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=105]]); from 3 January 2018, "undue additional operational risk" ([[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=108]], Annex A 2.6).
- **Due skill, care and diligence across the life of the arrangement:** when entering into, managing or terminating it (SYSC 8.1.7R, [[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=106]]).
- **Conditions the firm must secure** (SYSC 8.1.8R, [[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=106]], [[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=107]]), among them:
  - the provider has the ability, capacity and any authorisation needed to perform reliably and professionally
  - the firm has methods to assess the provider's performance
  - the provider supervises the work and manages its risks
  - the firm keeps the expertise to supervise the functions and manage the risks itself
  - the firm can terminate without detriment to continuity and quality of service
  - the firm, its auditors and the regulator have access to data and premises
  - both have a disaster-recovery contingency plan, with periodic testing of backup facilities
- **In writing.** Rights and obligations must be clearly allocated in a written agreement (SYSC 8.1.9R, [[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=107]]). From 3 January 2018 the MiFID Org Regulation adds that sub-outsourcing needs the firm's written consent (Art. 31(3), [[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=112]]).
- **Within a group.** When provider and firm are in the same group, the firm may take into account how far it controls or influences the provider (SYSC 8.1.10R, [[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=108]]).
- **Applied: TSB and SABIS.** The FCA found that [[wiki/entities/TSB Bank]] breached Principle 2 because, among other things, it never formally assessed whether SABIS, a Sabadell group company ([[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=27]], para 4.53), had the ability and capacity to deliver and run its new platform, and got too little assurance on SABIS's 85 fourth parties and on its readiness ([[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=93]], [[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=94]], para 5.3; [[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=76]], para 4.247). It had no contract with the fourth parties but kept its regulatory responsibility for them ([[raw/fca-final-notice-tsb-2022.pdf#page=76]], para 4.247).
