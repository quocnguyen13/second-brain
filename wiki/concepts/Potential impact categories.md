# Potential impact categories

The PRA's four categories ranking deposit-takers and designated investment firms by how much harm their failure or unsafe conduct could do to UK financial stability; the starting point of every firm's risk assessment ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=20]]).

## What the sources say
- **The four categories** ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=20]]):
  - **Category 1:** the most significant firms, able to cause very significant disruption to the UK financial system
  - **Category 2:** significant firms, able to cause some disruption
  - **Category 3:** able to cause minor disruption, though sector-wide difficulties could generate disruption
  - **Category 4:** almost no capacity individually to cause disruption, though sector-wide difficulties could
- **Five until 2023.** The PRA moved from five to four categories in 2023 ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=20]]).
- **How firms are placed.** Numerical scoring from regulatory reporting gives an indicated category, which supervisors review with qualitative analysis. The process is reviewed annually, and firms are told their category as an indication of how much supervision to expect ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=20]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=21]]).
- **What it drives.** The intensity of supervision mainly reflects potential impact, proximity to failure, resolvability and statutory obligations ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=18]]). Low-impact firms get a standard PSM letter for their peer group; others an individual letter ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=47]]).
- **Category 4 in practice.** Credit unions are the main constituent of the lowest category and are supervised on a portfolio basis, examined individually only when an alert or risk arises ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=53]]).
