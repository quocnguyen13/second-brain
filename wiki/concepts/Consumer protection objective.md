# Consumer protection objective

This is the FCA's operational objective of "securing an appropriate degree of protection for consumers" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1C(1)).

## What the sources say
- **What the FCA must weigh.** In deciding what degree of protection is appropriate, the FCA must have regard to ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1C(2)):
  - the differing risks of different investments and transactions
  - consumers' differing experience and expertise
  - consumers' need for timely, accurate, fit-for-purpose information and advice
  - the principle that consumers "should take responsibility for their decisions"
  - the principle that providers should give a level of care that fits the risk and the consumers' capabilities
  - consumers' differing expectations
  - information from the ombudsman scheme
- **Who counts as a consumer.** "Consumers" is broad ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1G):
  - anyone who uses, has used or may use regulated financial services
  - anyone with rights or interests derived from those services
  - investors in financial instruments
  - people affected by regulated benchmarks
  - targets of claims-management activity

  It is not limited to retail customers.
- **Its place among the objectives.** It sits beside integrity and competition. The FCA's competition duty yields to it where the two conflict ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1B(4)).
- **A shared principle.** "Consumers should take responsibility for their decisions" is also one of the [[wiki/concepts/Regulatory principles]] that bind both regulators ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3B(1)(d)).
- **The Consumer Panel.** Consumer representation runs through the FCA's Consumer Panel. It must give a fair degree of representation to people using services outside their business ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1Q(5)).
