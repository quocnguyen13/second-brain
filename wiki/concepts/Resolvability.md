# Resolvability

How much assurance there is that a firm can fail in an orderly way: without excessive disruption to the financial system, without interrupting critical functions, and without exposing public funds ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=37]]).

## What the sources say
- **Failure is allowed.** The PRA is not required to ensure that no firm fails ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2G). It does not run a zero-failure regime; firms should be able to fail without public funds at risk, since public support creates moral hazard ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=11]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=12]]).
- **Who does what.** The Bank of England is the resolution authority and sets the preferred resolution strategy for every firm; the PRA works with it ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=11]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=37]]).
- **Three strategies:** bail-in of shareholders and creditors; transfer to a private purchaser or bridge bank; modified insolvency ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=37]]).
- **Smaller deposit-takers.** Failure is usually through insolvency, with covered depositors paid by the FSCS or transferred. Firms must produce a Single Customer View so the FSCS can pay out, within a target of seven days for most customers ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=37]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=38]]).
- **Larger firms.** Disorderly failure is likely to be systemic, so their strategy is likely to use a transfer or bail-in tool ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=38]]).
- **Resolvability Assessment Framework.** Firms must be able to have adequate financial resources in resolution, keep doing business through resolution, and co-ordinate and communicate effectively. Certain firms assess and report on their own preparations and publish a summary; the Bank publishes a statement; the cycle runs every two years ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=39]]).
- **Removing impediments.** The Bank can direct a firm to remove a substantive impediment. The firm has four months to propose; if that is insufficient the Bank must direct, and the firm then has one month to propose a plan ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=40]]).
- **Solvent exit and recovery planning.** The PRA prefers non-systemic firms to exit before insolvency. Recovery planning is a prescribed responsibility under the Senior Managers Regime ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=37]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=38]]).
- **Competition effect.** More resolvable banks reduce the implicit subsidy to banking groups and encourage effective competition ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=37]]).
- **At authorisation.** A new bank's application includes whether it could be resolved in an orderly way ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=42]]).
