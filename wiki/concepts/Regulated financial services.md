# Regulated financial services

This is the statutory list of services that sets the reach of the FCA's consumer, competition and strategic objectives. It is defined by who provides a service and in what capacity, not by product name ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1H(2)).

## What the sources say
- **The list.** "Regulated financial services" means services provided by ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1H(2)):
  - (a) authorised persons carrying on regulated activities
  - (c) authorised persons communicating or approving financial promotions, meaning invitations or inducements to engage in investment activity or claims management
  - (d) investment firms and qualifying credit institutions providing relevant ancillary services
  - (e) appointed representatives
  - (f) payment service providers providing payment services
  - (g) electronic money issuers issuing electronic money
  - (h) sponsors to issuers of securities
  - (i) primary information providers
- **Where it matters.**
  - The markets for these services are among the FCA's "relevant markets" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1F(b)).
  - Their users are "consumers" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1G(1)(a)).
  - The competition objective covers their markets ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1E(1)(a)).
- **Amendment.** The Treasury can amend the list by order ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1J(c)).
- **Product lines named or implied.**
  - Payments and e-money are named directly: (f) and (g).
  - Consumer credit comes in through regulated activities: the Consumer Credit Order 2013 applies the FCA's supervision duties in s. 1L to it ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1L notes C18–C19).
- **Financial crime.** The FCA must also have regard to minimising the use of firms for financial crime, defined to include fraud, handling the proceeds of crime and terrorist financing ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 1B(5)(b), 1H(3)).
