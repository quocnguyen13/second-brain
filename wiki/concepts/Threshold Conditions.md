# Threshold Conditions

The minimum requirements a firm must meet at all times to be permitted to carry on its regulated activities ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=12]]).

## What the sources say
- **Purpose.** They are designed to ensure firms conduct business prudently and are run by people with adequate skills, experience and propriety. The PRA expects firms to meet the letter and also to consider the overriding principle of safety and soundness, and assesses firms against them continually ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=12]]).
- **Link to the Fundamental Rules.** A failure to comply with the PRA's Fundamental Rules may be relevant to a firm's ongoing compliance with the Threshold Conditions ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=13]]).
- **At authorisation.** A new deposit-taker is authorised only if both the PRA and the FCA are satisfied it will meet each regulator's Threshold Conditions, at authorisation and on an ongoing basis ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=42]]). See [[wiki/concepts/New bank authorisation]].
- **Each regulator has its own.** On a dual-regulated firm's application, each regulator assesses it against the relevant Threshold Conditions in line with its objectives ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=21]]). With-profits: the PRA's concern is that discretionary increases in liabilities don't threaten an insurer's ability to meet its Threshold Conditions ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=9]]).
- **Branches and subsidiaries.** Branches and subsidiaries of overseas banks meet the same Threshold Conditions, though how they meet them varies with their structure ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=55]]).
- **In distress.** PIF stage 4 is reached when there is a real risk the firm will fail to meet the Threshold Conditions ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=46]]).
- **In the Act.** "The threshold conditions" are the conditions set out in or under Schedule 6 of FSMA, read with any threshold condition code either regulator makes under s. 137O. Each regulator is responsible for the conditions expressed to be relevant to its functions ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=84]], s. 55B(1)–(2)).
- **The statutory duty.** In giving or varying a [[wiki/concepts/Part 4A permission]], imposing or varying a requirement, or giving consent, each regulator must ensure the firm "will satisfy, and continue to satisfy" the threshold conditions it is responsible for, for every activity it has or will have permission for ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=84]], s. 55B(3)). The duty doesn't stop a regulator acting to advance its objectives ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=84]], s. 55B(4)). Section 55B(3) is excluded for specified purposes from 15 July 2025 (fully from 15 July 2026) by S.I. 2025/859 ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=85]], C149).
- **Failing them is a ground for action.** Either regulator may vary or cancel a permission, or impose a requirement, on its own initiative where a firm is failing, or likely to fail, the threshold conditions it is responsible for ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=90]], s. 55J(1)(a); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=97]], s. 55L(2)(a); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=98]], s. 55M(2)(a)).
- **Who can change them.** The Treasury may amend Parts 1 and 2 of Schedule 6 by order ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=85]], s. 55C(1)). For a non-UK firm, a regulator may weigh the home regulator's opinion on whether it meets them ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=85]], s. 55D(2)–(3)).
