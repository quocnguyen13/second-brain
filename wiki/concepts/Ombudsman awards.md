# Ombudsman awards

An Ombudsman award is what the Financial Ombudsman Service can order when it upholds a complaint: a money award up to a cap, interest, costs, or a direction to the firm ([[raw/fca-disp-3-7-ombudsman-awards.md]]).

## What the sources say
- **In the Act (emphasis).** Under the compulsory jurisdiction, a determination in the complainant's favour may include ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=602]], s. 229(1)–(2)):
  - a money award, of what the ombudsman considers fair compensation for financial loss, or for other loss or damage of a kind specified in compulsory jurisdiction rules ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=602]], s. 229(3); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=603]], s. 229(11));
  - a direction to take steps the ombudsman considers just and appropriate, "whether or not a court could order those steps".
- **The limit in the Act.** A money award may not exceed the monetary limit, which is "such amount as may be specified" in compulsory jurisdiction rules. Different amounts may be set for different kinds of complaint. Above the limit, the ombudsman may only recommend that the firm pays the balance ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=602]], s. 229(5)–(6); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=603]], s. 229(7), (11)). The Act gives no figure: the figures are DISP's, under "The cap" below.
- **Enforcement in the Act (emphasis).** A money award may carry interest at a rate and from a date set in the award, and the complainant can enforce it under Part III of Schedule 17. A direction can be enforced by injunction, or in Scotland by an order under s. 45 of the Court of Session Act 1988. Only the complainant may bring those proceedings ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=603]], s. 229(8)–(10)). A determination binds the firm only once the complainant accepts it ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=601]], s. 228(5)–(6)); see [[wiki/entities/Financial Ombudsman Service]].
- **Costs in the Act.** Costs rules are made by the scheme operator with the FCA's approval. They can't make the complainant pay the firm's costs, only contribute to the scheme operator's costs, and only where the complainant's conduct was improper or unreasonable or caused unreasonable delay ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=604]], s. 230(1)–(4)).
- **Money.** Fair compensation for financial loss, pain and suffering, damage to reputation, and distress or inconvenience, "whether or not a court would award compensation" ([[raw/fca-disp-3-7-ombudsman-awards.md]], Money awards).
- **The cap.** For complaints referred on or after 1 April 2026: £455,000 for acts on or after 1 April 2019, and £205,000 for earlier acts. The cap rises with CPI each 1 April, rounded down to £5,000 ([[raw/fca-disp-3-7-ombudsman-awards.md]]).
- **Outside the cap.** Interest and costs don't count towards it. Above it, the Ombudsman can only recommend that the firm pays the balance ([[raw/fca-disp-3-7-ombudsman-awards.md]]).
- **Directions.** The firm can be told to take steps the Ombudsman considers just and appropriate, even ones a court couldn't order ([[raw/fca-disp-3-7-ombudsman-awards.md]], Directions).
- **Costs.** These are rare, because complainants "should not need to have professional advisers" ([[raw/fca-disp-3-7-ombudsman-awards.md]]).
- **Binding on the firm.** It must comply promptly, including with settlements it agreed earlier, and the complainant can enforce a registered money award or a direction in the courts ([[raw/fca-disp-3-7-ombudsman-awards.md]]).
