# General prohibition

The FSMA rule that no person may carry on a regulated activity in the UK, or purport to, unless they are authorised or exempt ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=51]], s. 19).

## What the sources say
- **The rule.** No person may carry on a regulated activity in the United Kingdom, "or purport to do so", unless they are an authorised person or an exempt person ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=51]], s. 19(1)). The Act refers to it as "the general prohibition" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=51]], s. 19(2)).
- **What it bites on.** A regulated activity is an activity of a kind the Treasury specifies by order, carried on by way of business, relating to a specified investment or, for some activities, to property of any kind ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=56]], s. 22(1); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=57]], s. 22(5)). So the Treasury, by its orders, sets what needs authorisation ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=51]], s. 19(1); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=56]], s. 22(1)).
- **Authorised firms outside their permission** are not caught by s. 19, since they are authorised. Instead they are treated as breaching a requirement imposed by the FCA, or by the FCA and the PRA for a PRA-authorised person ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=52]], s. 20(1), (1A)).
- **Breaching it is an offence.** Up to six months' imprisonment or a fine on summary conviction, up to two years or a fine on indictment; due-diligence defence ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=60]], s. 23(1); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=61]], s. 23(3)). Claiming to be authorised or exempt when one isn't is a separate offence ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=62]], s. 24(1)).
- **Customers can walk away.** An agreement made in breach of it is unenforceable against the customer, who can recover what they paid plus compensation; so is one an authorised firm makes in consequence of an unauthorised intermediary's activity ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=63]], s. 26(1)–(2); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=65]], s. 27(1A)–(2)). The court may still let it be enforced if just and equitable, except for credit, where the FCA decides ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=66]], s. 28(1), (3); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=67]], s. 28A(3)).
- **Deposits.** Unauthorised deposit-taking doesn't make the deposit agreement unenforceable; instead the depositor can ask the court to order the money returned ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=63]], s. 26(4); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=68]], s. 29(1)–(2)).
- **In the FCA's remit.** Services that unauthorised persons provide without breaching the general prohibition are one of the FCA's "relevant markets" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1F(c)).
