## Summary
This is the FCA's own "About" page. It covers what the [[wiki/entities/Financial Conduct Authority]] regulates and how many firms that covers. It also covers the FCA's origins, objectives, funding and accountability, and how it works: it makes rules, authorises firms, supervises them and enforces ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]). The objectives it lists match the statute in [[wiki/sources/Source - FSMA 2000 Part 1A]]. What this page adds is the regulator's own account of its scale and its methods.

## Key claims

### Firm numbers, and what each one counts
The page gives these as approximate figures. They were last updated on 2026-07-09, after the Annual Report was published ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).

| Figure | What it counts |
|---|---|
| ~35,500 | Firms whose **conduct** the FCA regulates |
| ~34,000 | Firms the FCA **prudentially supervises** |
| ~14,000 | Firms **subject to the prudential standards in the FCA Handbook** |
| ~1,500 | Banks, building societies, credit unions, insurers and major investment firms the **PRA** regulates prudentially. **The FCA states this figure**, not the PRA |

All rows cite [[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]].

### How the FCA regulates
- **Rules and market work.** It makes rules and issues guidance and standards. It uses market studies to find harm across a whole market and to put remedies in place ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- **Authorise.** Firms and individuals must be authorised or registered by the FCA before they carry on regulated activities. A firm must show it meets a range of requirements before it is authorised ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- **Supervise.** After authorisation, the FCA supervises a firm to check that it keeps meeting its standards and rules ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]). Supervision is proportionate: the FCA gives priority to the firms and areas that pose a higher risk to its objectives, weighing size, complexity and the potential impact on different types of consumer ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]). It uses data to spot problems and act faster against firms and individuals likely to cause harm ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- **Enforce.** When firms or individuals fail to meet its standards, it takes action with a range of enforcement powers, including criminal prosecutions for financial crime ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- **Financial crime.** It works with partners to reduce and prevent financial crime ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).

### Who it is
- **Origins.** The page says it was "established on 1 April 2013", taking over conduct and relevant prudential regulation from the Financial Services Authority (FSA) ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]). In law, it is the FSA renamed ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1A(1)); see Conflicts below.
- **Status and funding.** It is an independent public body, "funded entirely by the fees we charge regulated firms" ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- **Accountability.** Its role and objectives are "primarily defined by" FSMA 2000. It is accountable to [[wiki/entities/HM Treasury]] and to Parliament ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- **Offices.** Its head office is in London, with offices in Leeds and Edinburgh and staff in Belfast and Cardiff ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).

### Objectives
- **Strategic objective.** Relevant markets function well ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- **Operational objectives.**
  - protect consumers
  - protect the integrity of the UK financial system
  - promote effective competition in the interests of consumers

  ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]). See [[wiki/concepts/Regulatory objectives]].
- **Secondary objective.** Since 2023 it has had to facilitate the UK economy's international competitiveness and its growth in the medium to long term, subject to alignment with international standards ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- **Principles.** It considers the principles of good regulation in its work ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]). See [[wiki/concepts/Regulatory principles]].

### Working with others and measuring itself
- **The PRA.** It works with the [[wiki/entities/Prudential Regulation Authority]] to advance its objectives ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- **Self-assessed value.** The FCA estimates that it adds at least £14 of benefit to society for every £1 of its running costs. The estimate is based on its policy and enforcement work ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- **Reporting.** It publishes the following ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]):
  - an annual work programme and an Annual Report
  - multi-year outcomes and metrics for 2022–2025 and 2025–2030
  - operating service metrics
  - quarterly authorisation metrics

## Conflicts and open points
- **Established or renamed (scope).** This page says the FCA was "established on 1 April 2013", taking over from the FSA. FSMA says the FCA is the body formerly called the Financial Services Authority, renamed ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1A(1)). Both are primary. Not a conflict once scoped: the Act gives the legal identity, this page the operating start. Resolved 2026-10-06, [[system/ingest/set-2026-10-06]] conflict 1; see [[wiki/entities/Financial Conduct Authority]].
- **The prudential figures aren't explained.** The page doesn't say how the ~34,000 firms the FCA "prudentially supervises" relate to the ~14,000 "subject to the prudential standards in our Handbook".
- **The figures may reconcile, but the page doesn't say so.** 35,500 − 34,000 = 1,500, the same as the PRA figure. The page never states that the FCA prudentially supervises every conduct-regulated firm except the PRA's. A PRA source would settle this.
- **The PRA figure is second-hand.** The FCA states it, not the PRA.
- **Self-reported.** The £14-per-£1 estimate is the FCA's own estimate of its impact.
- **Clip quality.** The clip looks complete. The link text is split ("[P] [rotect consumers]"), and one change-log entry has no date.
