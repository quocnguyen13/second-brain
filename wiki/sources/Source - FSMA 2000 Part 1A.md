**Raw file:** [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]] · **Author or publisher:** UK Parliament, via legislation.gov.uk · **Published:** Act of 2000. Part 1A was substituted by the Financial Services Act 2012 and took effect in 2013. ## Summary
Part 1A of the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 sets up the UK's two financial regulators ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]]):
- the [[wiki/entities/Financial Conduct Authority]] (Chapter 1)
- the [[wiki/entities/Prudential Regulation Authority]] (Chapter 2)
- the rules that bind the two together (Chapter 3)

Each regulator gets objectives, "general functions", supervision duties, consultation panels and review mechanisms. The FCA covers every authorised person; the PRA covers only PRA-authorised persons. Firms in the PRA's population therefore sit under both regulators (see [[wiki/concepts/Solo and dual regulation]]). Chapter 3 sets out how the two coordinate, how the PRA can veto FCA action, and how [[wiki/entities/HM Treasury]] can redraw the boundary between them.

## Key claims

### Who is regulated by whom (single vs dual)
- **The FCA supervises every authorised person.** It must maintain arrangements for supervising authorised persons. It must also monitor, and enforce against, certain persons who are *not* authorised ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1L).
- **The PRA supervises only PRA-authorised persons** ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2K).
- **One PRA-regulated activity is enough.** A "PRA-authorised person" is an authorised person whose permission covers regulated activities that "consist of or include one or more PRA-regulated activities". Which activities those are is left to s. 22A, which this clip doesn't include ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2B(5)).
- **What follows:** an authorised person with any PRA-regulated activity answers to both regulators, and one without answers to the FCA alone. The Act doesn't use the words "solo-regulated" or "dual-regulated" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 1L, 2K, 2B(5)).
- **The Treasury controls who falls under the PRA.** It can make activities PRA-regulated by order under s. 22A, and give the PRA an additional objective for them ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2D). It can also specify which matters are, or are mainly, one regulator's responsibility for PRA-authorised persons ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3G).

### How the FCA and PRA differ in scope
| | FCA | PRA |
|---|---|---|
| Who | The body formerly called the Financial Services Authority, renamed (s. 1A) | The Bank of England, acting through its Prudential Regulation Committee (s. 2A) |
| Population | All authorised persons, plus some non-authorised persons for compliance (s. 1L) | PRA-authorised persons (ss. 2B(5), 2K) |
| Top objective | Strategic: the relevant markets "function well" (s. 1B(2)) | General: "the safety and soundness of PRA-authorised persons" (s. 2B(2)) |
| Other objectives | Operational: consumer protection, integrity, competition (s. 1B(3)) | Insurance: protection for policyholders (s. 2C). Ring-fencing: continuity of core services (s. 2B(3)(c)) |
| Secondary objectives | Competitiveness and growth (s. 1B(4A)) | Competition, and competitiveness and growth (s. 2H(1)) |
| Competition | A duty to promote effective competition in consumers' interests, as far as compatible with consumer protection and integrity (s. 1B(4)) | A secondary objective to *facilitate* effective competition (s. 2H(1A)) |
| Failure | Not addressed | Not required to ensure that no PRA-authorised person fails (s. 2G) |
| General functions | Rules, technical standards, codes, general guidance, general policy (s. 1B(6)) | Rules, technical standards, codes, general policy (s. 2J(1)) |
| Panels | Practitioner, Smaller Business Practitioner, Markets Practitioner, Consumer, Listing Authority Advisory (ss. 1N–1QA) | PRA Practitioner, Insurance Practitioner (ss. 2M–2MA) |

All cells cite [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]] at the section shown. See [[wiki/concepts/Regulatory objectives]].

### What matters for a product owner, by product
The Act doesn't regulate products by name. It defines "regulated financial services" by who provides them and in what capacity (s. 1H(2); see [[wiki/concepts/Regulated financial services]]):
- **Payments.** Payment service providers providing payment services count as "regulated financial services", so they fall within the FCA's consumer and competition objectives ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1H(2)(f)). The Payment Services Regulations 2017 and the E-Money Regulations 2011 apply the Treasury-recommendations mechanism (s. 1JA), with modifications, to their own regimes ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1JA notes C14–C15).
- **E-money and prepaid cards.** Issuing electronic money counts as a regulated financial service ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1H(2)(g)).
- **Lending and cards (consumer credit).** The Financial Services Act 2012 (Consumer Credit) Order 2013 applies the FCA's supervision and monitoring duties in s. 1L(1)–(2), with modifications ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1L notes C18–C19). More generally, any service an authorised person provides while carrying on a regulated activity is a regulated financial service ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1H(2)(a)).
- **Every consumer product.** The [[wiki/concepts/Consumer protection objective]] makes the FCA weigh the following ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1C(2)):
  - the risk of the product
  - the consumer's experience
  - the consumer's need for timely, accurate information and advice
  - the principle that consumers take responsibility for their decisions
  - the level of care firms owe, given the risk and the consumer's capabilities
- **Onboarding and switching.** When the FCA judges competition it may look at ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1E(2)):
  - information that enables informed choice
  - access, including in deprived areas
  - how easily consumers can switch provider
  - how easily new entrants can get in
  - innovation
- **Financial crime, and so onboarding checks.** The FCA must have regard to minimising the use of firms for financial crime ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1B(5)(b)). "Financial crime" includes fraud, handling the proceeds of crime and terrorist financing ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1H(3)).
- **Accountability.** Both regulators must have regard to the responsibilities of senior management for compliance ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3B(1)(e)).

### Mechanics of FCA–PRA coordination
See [[wiki/concepts/FCA-PRA coordination]]. Briefly:
- **A duty to coordinate.** Each regulator must consult the other before acting in a way that may have a material adverse effect on the other's objectives. Each must seek the other's information and advice on matters of common regulatory interest. The duty is limited by compatibility with each regulator's objectives and by proportionality ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3D).
- **A memorandum of understanding.** It must be published, reviewed at least once each calendar year, and sent to the Treasury, which lays it before Parliament ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3E). A separate or combined memorandum covers with-profits insurers ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3F).
- **The PRA's veto.** The PRA can direct the FCA not to use a power, or not to use it in a specified way, if it thinks the proposed use may threaten financial stability, cause a damaging failure, or threaten core services ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3I). It has a parallel power for with-profits policies ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3J). Before using either, it must consult the FCA and give written reasons. Directions are published unless that is against the public interest ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3L).
- **Consolidated supervision of groups.** Whichever regulator is the consolidated supervisor can direct the other on authorised members of the group ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 3M–3P).
- **Consultation on guidance.** Each regulator consults the other before issuing guidance on its objectives ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 1K(2), 2I(2)).
- **Shared services.** The regulators may provide services to each other ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3R(1)).

### Accountability to the Treasury and Parliament
- **Recommendations on economic policy.** The Treasury must make them to the FCA at least once each Parliament. The FCA must answer within 12 months and then update the Treasury every year ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1JA).
- **Reviews.** The Treasury can commission an independent review of how economically, efficiently and effectively the FCA uses its resources ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1S).
- **Rules.** Both regulators must keep their rules under review ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3RA). The Treasury can direct a review of specified rules ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 3RC–3RD). It can also require rules to be made on a specified activity, but not with specified content ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3RE).

## Conflicts and open points
- No conflicts with other sources. This is the first financial-regulation source.
- **Which activities are PRA-regulated is not in this source.** Section 22A and its order are needed to say which firms are dual-regulated.
- **The clip shows two versions of s. 3B(1)(c).** One is "sustainable growth"; the other is the net-zero and environmental targets. The amendment note says the substitution came in "for specified purposes" in 2023 and 2025. Which version applies to which functions is unclear from the clip.
- **The clip has formatting losses.** Several inserted sections lost their numbers in the headings (1EB, 1MA, 1QA, 1RA, 2A, 2H, 2LA, 2MA, 2NA, 3RE); this page names them from the amendment notes. The s. 1H(8) definitions are empty.
- The text is a point-in-time consolidation. legislation.gov.uk notes changes not yet in force.
