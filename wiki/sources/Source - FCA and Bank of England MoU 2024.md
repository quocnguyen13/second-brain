# Source - FCA and Bank of England MoU 2024

**Raw file:** [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf]] · **Author or publisher:** Financial Conduct Authority and Bank of England (exercising its prudential regulation functions) · **Published:** signed 2024-03-26; replaces the MoU adopted on 2 July 2019 · **Trust:** primary (the regulators' own publication) · **Ingested:** 2026-10-06

Page citations use the PDF's page numbers, which match the printed ones.

## Summary
The high-level framework the FCA and the PRA use to co-ordinate and co-operate, agreed under FSMA ss. 3D and 3E ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=1]]). It restates each regulator's objectives, then sets out who leads on what: policy and rule-making, permissions, senior managers, change in control, supervision of dual-regulated firms, enforcement, the PRA's power of direction, fees, cross-board seats, information sharing and confidentiality. Annex 1 gives the consent, consultation and notification steps for each regulatory process; Annex 2 covers regulatory data.

## Key claims

### Mandates
- **Very different mandates.** The FCA is responsible for "maintaining the integrity of the provision of financial services to users". The Bank is responsible for monetary policy and financial stability, crisis management, oversight of payment and settlement systems and central counterparties, and is the resolution authority; as the PRA it promotes the safety and soundness of deposit-takers, designated investment firms and insurers ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=1]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=2]]).
- **What the FCA is responsible for** ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=3]]): conduct in retail and wholesale markets; trading infrastructure; prudential supervision of firms that are not PRA-regulated; its functions as securities regulator.
- **What the PRA is responsible for.** Authorisation, in conjunction with the FCA, and prudential supervision of deposit-takers (banks, building societies, credit unions), insurers (including friendly societies) and designated investment firms ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=4]]).
- **Objectives** of both regulators, including ring-fencing under the PRA's general objective, restated as in FSMA ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=2]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=3]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=4]]).

### Who leads on what (emphasis)
- **Lead regulator.** The PRA leads for dual-regulated firms ("authorised by the PRA and regulated by both"); the FCA leads for solo-regulated firms ("authorised and regulated by the FCA only") and designated activities. The lead runs a single administrative process and copies the other regulator in ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=6]]).
- **Permissions.** For dual-regulated firms the PRA must obtain the FCA's **consent** before granting or removing a permission or adding a regulated activity, and consult it before cancelling. Each regulator assesses against its own Threshold Conditions ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=21]]). Consent carries a veto ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=21]]).
- **Senior managers.** Lead responsibility splits by which regulator's rules designate the SMF. The PRA leads its SMFs with FCA consent; the FCA alone approves SMFs tied to the customer interface, such as client assets, anti-money laundering and compliance ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=6]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=21]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=22]]).
- **Change in control.** The target's prudential regulator leads; for a dual-regulated target the PRA must consult the FCA, and the FCA may direct the PRA to object on money-laundering or terrorist-financing grounds ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=22]]).
- **Register.** The FCA keeps a single register of all FCA- and PRA-authorised firms and senior managers ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=6]]).
- **Supervision.** Not normally joint: each regulator's risk assessment reflects its own objectives ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=6]]). The PRA routinely shares recovery-plan conclusions, the firm's PIF position, and capital and liquidity details; the FCA shares key conduct-risk findings and material prudential risks in subsidiaries ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=7]]). Domestic supervisory colleges meet as appropriate; categorisation is reviewed annually by the heads of supervision ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=7]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=8]]).
- **FSCS.** The PRA makes FSCS rules for depositor and policyholder protection; the FCA for other activities ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=11]]).
- **Ring-fencing transfers.** The PRA leads Part VII transfers for banking and ring-fencing schemes; the FCA reviews the notices sent to consumers ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=24]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=25]]).

### Enforcement and directions
- **Investigations.** Each notifies the other of material investigations into relevant firms early and before enforcement action; where both objectives are engaged they decide who investigates, or whether jointly ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=12]]).
- **Unauthorised activity.** The FCA investigates unauthorised regulated activity (s. 168) and notifies the PRA if it involves deposit-taking or insurance ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=14]]).
- **PRA power of direction (s. 3I).** The final decision is taken by the PRC, after a meeting between the FCA CEO and the Deputy Governor for Prudential Regulation ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=16]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=17]]). It is not available against the FCA's concurrent competition powers ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=16]]).

### Governance and money
- **Fees.** Each regulator is funded by fees on the regulated community; the FCA may collect fees for the PRA under a separate agreement ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=17]]).
- **Cross-board seats.** The FCA CEO sits ex officio on the PRC and the Deputy Governor for Prudential Regulation on the FCA Board, under the "Twin Peaks" architecture ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=18]]).
- **Regulatory Initiatives Forum.** Co-chaired by the FCA and the Bank/PRA since 2020 ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=18]]).
- **Review.** Co-ordination is reviewed every half-year, with a summary in each regulator's annual report ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=19]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=20]]).

## Conflicts and open points
- None with other sources.
- **Review frequency.** FSMA requires the MoU itself to be reviewed at least once a calendar year ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3E(4)). This MoU describes half-yearly reviews of co-ordination ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=19]]). They are different things, so not a conflict. The 2024 MoU replaced one from 2019, so the wiki doesn't show yearly reissues.
