# Source - DISP 2.7 Complainant eligibility

**Raw file:** [[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]] · **Author or publisher:** Financial Conduct Authority, FCA Handbook (DISP 2.7, "Is the complainant eligible?") · **Published:** last updated 2025-11-28; a future version is dated 2027-10-25; viewed as of 2026-10-12, the clip date · **Trust:** primary (a regulator's own rules) · **Ingested:** 2026-10-14

## Summary
These are the FCA Handbook rules on who can take a complaint to the [[wiki/entities/Financial Ombudsman Service]]. A complainant must pass two tests. The first is who they are: a consumer, a micro-enterprise, a small business, a small charity or trust, a CBTL consumer or a guarantor. The second is that the complaint must arise from a listed relationship with the firm. The section adds rules for business groups, for firms in doubt, for APP fraud victims and for politically exposed persons, and it excludes firms and professional clients ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]). See [[wiki/concepts/Eligible complainant]].

## Key claims
- **Only eligible complainants.** The FOS deals with a complaint only if it is brought by or on behalf of an eligible complainant. The person acting for them doesn't have to be eligible ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]).
- **Who is eligible** ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]], Eligible complainants):
  - a consumer;
  - a **micro-enterprise**. For a payment-services complaint, it must be one either when the payment-service contract was made or when it complains to the firm; otherwise, when it complains to the firm;
  - a charity with annual income under £6.5 million when it complains to the firm;
  - a trustee of a trust with net assets under £5 million;
  - a CBTL consumer, for CBTL business;
  - a **small business** when it complains to the firm;
  - a guarantor.
- **Groups count.** The micro-enterprise and small-business tests take account of "partner enterprises" and "linked enterprises", as defined in the Micro-enterprise Recommendation. If a parent company with a majority holding fails the tests, so does the complainant ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]).
- **When in doubt, treat as eligible.** A firm in doubt about whether a business, charity or trust is eligible should treat it as eligible. The Ombudsman decides on evidence such as audited accounts or VAT returns ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]).
- **A relationship with the firm.** The complaint must also arise from a listed relationship ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]], DISP 2.7.6R). Those that matter most for a bank:
  - customer, payment service user or e-money holder, or a potential one (1), (2);
  - payer, where the firm is the payee's payment service provider, on its duties under PSR regulation 90(3) (2A);
  - someone who sent money in an alleged **authorised push payment fraud**, where the firm was involved in the transfer and the complaint is not a PSD complaint (2B). This includes a complaint that the firm didn't do enough to prevent or respond to the fraud ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]);
  - true owner of a cheque collected for someone else (8);
  - recipient of a banker's reference (9);
  - giver of a guarantee or security for a mortgage, loan or regulated credit agreement (10). A guarantor is eligible only for complaints arising from this relationship;
  - the subject of credit-reference or credit information (11), (11A), or of specified information given in a finance application (11B);
  - a person the firm has pursued for payment under a credit agreement, or dealt with in debt administration (12).
- **Politically exposed persons.** An individual whom the firm identified as a PEP, a family member of one or a known close associate is eligible to complain that the identification is wrong, or about what the firm did because of it ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]).
- **Who is excluded** ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]], DISP 2.7.9R):
  - firms, payment service providers, e-money issuers and others, on activities they themselves carry on;
  - in the Compulsory Jurisdiction, a professional client or eligible counterparty in relation to the firm and activity in question, other than a pension-scheme trustee.

  Neither exclusion applies to a complainant who is a consumer for the activity complained of.
- **Transitional complaints.** Older complaints that would have gone to schemes before the FOS, such as the Insurance Ombudsman, GISC and MCAS, have their own eligibility tests. The Ombudsman may treat a complainant as eligible if they could have used the former scheme ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]).

## Conflicts and open points
- **Definitions are elsewhere.** "Consumer", "micro-enterprise" and "small business" are Glossary terms. This clip links them but doesn't define them. The two business terms are compiled from [[wiki/sources/Source - FCA glossary small business]] and [[wiki/sources/Source - FCA glossary micro-enterprise]]. "Consumer" is not yet in the wiki.
- **A future version.** A future version of the section is dated 2027-10-25. Its content isn't in the clip.
- **Fraud and scams (fact).** The FOS page puts all "fraud and scams" complaints on the 15-day clock ([[raw/financial-ombudsman-how-to-complain.md]]). DISP 2.7.6R(2B) recognises authorised push payment fraud complaints that are not PSD complaints, and only PSD and EMD complaints get the short deadline.
