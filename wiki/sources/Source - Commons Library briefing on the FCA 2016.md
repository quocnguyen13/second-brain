# Source - Commons Library briefing on the FCA 2016

**Raw file:** [[raw/edmonds-financial-conduct-authority-2016.pdf]] · **Author or publisher:** Timothy Edmonds, House of Commons Library · **Published:** Briefing Paper 7448, 2016-01-29 · **Trust:** secondary (parliamentary library briefing) · **Ingested:** 2026-10-06

Page citations use the PDF's page numbers, which match the printed ones.

## Summary
A four-page background note for a Commons debate on the FCA on 1 February 2016. It describes where the FCA came from, its objectives, accountability and four broad areas of work, then explains why the FCA is criticised most for failing to "sort out" scandals, with interest rate hedging products and the Connaught income fund as examples ([[raw/edmonds-financial-conduct-authority-2016.pdf#page=2]] · secondary). It is a snapshot from January 2016: it predates the FCA's 2023 secondary objective ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1B(4A), note F12) and says nothing on the PRA beyond one line.

## Key claims
- **Origins.** Section 6 of the Financial Services Act 2012 "established three new regulatory bodies" after the 2008 crisis; the FCA "assumed most of the functions" of the FSA, which had been heavily criticised for its supervisory failings pre-crisis ([[raw/edmonds-financial-conduct-authority-2016.pdf#page=2]] · secondary).
- **Dual regulation, in one line.** Some firms (deposit-takers) are regulated by the PRA for soundness and by the FCA for conduct ([[raw/edmonds-financial-conduct-authority-2016.pdf#page=2]] · secondary).
- **Objectives in 2016.** A strategic objective and three operational objectives, consumer protection, competition, and protecting and enhancing the UK financial system ([[raw/edmonds-financial-conduct-authority-2016.pdf#page=2]] · secondary). No secondary objective is mentioned: it came in 2023 ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1B(4A), note F12).
- **Independence and accountability.** Operationally independent, funded entirely by regulated firms, accountable to the Treasury and Parliament, "notably through regular appearances before the Treasury Select Committee" ([[raw/edmonds-financial-conduct-authority-2016.pdf#page=2]] · secondary).
- **Four headings of work:** assessing whether there are problems in the market; approving those who work in the industry; producing and implementing the rules; investigating breaches and punishing offenders ([[raw/edmonds-financial-conduct-authority-2016.pdf#page=2]] · secondary).
- **Where criticism falls.** Most often on the last heading, when the FCA can't sort out "scandals". Reasons offered: the regulator's scope has widened since FSMA 2000, often after a new scandal, giving it less leverage over activity that was outside scope when problems began; and complex, slow-maturing products make fault hard to establish ([[raw/edmonds-financial-conduct-authority-2016.pdf#page=2]], [[raw/edmonds-financial-conduct-authority-2016.pdf#page=3]] · secondary).
- **Interest rate hedging products.** Sold to many small businesses; banks said customers understood them, customers said the cover was a condition of their loan; £2.1 billion had been paid in compensation by January 2016 ([[raw/edmonds-financial-conduct-authority-2016.pdf#page=3]] · secondary).
- **Connaught income fund.** An insolvent collective investment scheme with a complex cross-border structure; in March 2015 the FCA withdrew from settlement talks and opened a formal investigation of two former operators ([[raw/edmonds-financial-conduct-authority-2016.pdf#page=3]] · secondary).

## Conflicts and open points
- **"Established" vs "renamed".** "Established three new regulatory bodies" sits beside FSMA's "renamed" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1A(1)). The same wording from the FCA was resolved by scope in [[system/ingest/set-2026-10-06]] conflict 1 (legal identity vs operating start), and this briefing's "assumed most of the functions" fits that reading. Recorded, not raised as a new conflict.
- **Dated.** January 2016. The objectives list predates the 2023 secondary objective ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1B(4A), note F12); the £2.1 billion figure is as of then.
