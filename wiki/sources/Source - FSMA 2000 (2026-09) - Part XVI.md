# Source - FSMA 2000 (2026-09) - Part XVI

**Raw file:** [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf]] · **Author or publisher:** UK Parliament, via legislation.gov.uk · **Published:** revised text up to date to 10 September 2026 · **Trust:** primary (legislation) · **Ingested:** 2026-10-20
**Part of:** [[wiki/sources/Source - FSMA 2000 (2026-09)]] · **Pages:** 596–609 · **Sections:** 225–234B

## Summary
Part XVI sets up the ombudsman scheme: a scheme for resolving certain disputes "quickly and with minimum formality by an independent person", run by a body corporate, the scheme operator ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=597]], s. 225(1)–(2)). It has a compulsory jurisdiction, which covers authorised persons, e-money issuers and payment service providers for activities named in FCA rules, and a voluntary jurisdiction for firms that join it ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=597]], s. 226(1)–(3); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=599]], s. 227(1)–(2)). Under the compulsory jurisdiction, the ombudsman decides on what is "fair and reasonable in all the circumstances of the case". If the complainant accepts the decision, it binds both parties and is final ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=601]], s. 228(2), (5)). A determination can include a money award, which is limited by FCA rules, and a direction, and both can be enforced by the complainant ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=602]], s. 229(2), (5)–(6); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=603]], s. 229(8)–(11); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=597]], s. 226(3)). The Part also gives the ombudsman powers to require information, and the FCA powers to fund the scheme through levies on firms ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=606]], s. 231; [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=608]], s. 234(1)).

## Key claims

### The scheme and its jurisdictions (ss. 225–227)
- **The scheme.** Certain disputes are to be resolved "quickly and with minimum formality by an independent person". A body corporate, the scheme operator, administers the scheme. The operator chooses the name it runs under, but the Act calls it "the ombudsman scheme". Schedule 17 makes further provision about the scheme and the operator ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=597]], s. 225(1)–(4)).
- **Other regimes that apply Part XVI.** The Payment Services Regulations 2009, the Electronic Money Regulations 2011, the Mortgage Credit Directive Order 2015, and the 2015 SME credit information and finance platforms regulations all apply Part XVI, some of them with modifications ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=596]], C964–C966; [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=597]], C967–C968).
- **Compulsory jurisdiction: when it applies.** A complaint about an act or omission in an activity covered by compulsory jurisdiction rules is to be dealt with under the scheme if three conditions are met ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=597]], s. 226(1)–(2)):
  - the complainant is eligible and wants the scheme to deal with it;
  - at the time of the act or omission, the respondent was an authorised person, an electronic money issuer under the Electronic Money Regulations 2011, or a payment service provider under the Payment Services Regulations 2017;
  - compulsory jurisdiction rules covered the activity at that time.
- **The FCA sets its scope.** Compulsory jurisdiction rules are made by the FCA and name the activities they apply to. Only regulated activities, or activities that could be made regulated by an order under s. 22, may be named ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=597]], s. 226(3)–(4)). Such an order is a Treasury order ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=57]], s. 22(5)). If the FCA names account information services provided by authorised payment institutions, it must name them to the same extent for registered account information service providers ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=597]], s. 226(5A)).
- **Eligibility is set by rules.** A complainant is eligible if they fall within a class the rules specify. The rules may make persons other than individuals eligible. They may make authorised persons eligible only in specified circumstances or for complaints of a specified kind ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=598]], s. 226(6)–(7)).
- **PEPs by statute.** The rules must make eligible a person whom a respondent has identified as a politically exposed person, a family member of one, or a known close associate of one. This covers a complaint that the identification was wrong, and a complaint about what the respondent did because of it ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=598]], s. 226(7A)–(7D)).
- **Consumer credit jurisdiction: omitted.** Section 226A was omitted with effect from 1 April 2014, so there is no separate consumer credit jurisdiction in the current text ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=599]], s. 226A, F1747).
- **Voluntary jurisdiction.** It covers complaints against a respondent that was participating in the scheme at the time of the act or omission and has not withdrawn by the time of referral. The activity must have been covered by voluntary jurisdiction rules, and the complaint must be one the compulsory jurisdiction can't deal with ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=599]], s. 227(1)–(2)). The scheme operator makes these rules, with the FCA's approval. They may cover only activities that are, or could be, named in compulsory jurisdiction rules ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=599]], s. 227(3)–(4); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=600]], s. 227(6)). Firms other than authorised persons may participate ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=600]], s. 227(10)).

### Determination of complaints (ss. 228–230A)
- **The test.** Under the compulsory jurisdiction, a complaint is determined by what is, "in the opinion of the ombudsman, fair and reasonable in all the circumstances of the case" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=601]], s. 228(1)–(2)).
- **The statement.** The ombudsman gives both parties a written statement that gives reasons, is signed, and requires the complainant to say by a specified date whether they accept or reject the determination ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=601]], s. 228(3)–(4)).
- **When it binds.** If the complainant accepts, the determination is "binding on the respondent and the complainant and final" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=601]], s. 228(5)). Silence by the date counts as rejection ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=601]], s. 228(6)). A late acceptance still counts if the complainant hasn't rejected the determination and conditions in the scheme operator's rules are met ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=601]], s. 228(6A)). The respondent is told the outcome. When silence was treated as rejection, the notice must describe the late-acceptance route ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=601]], s. 228(7)–(7A)).
- **Awards.** A determination in the complainant's favour may include a money award of what the ombudsman considers fair compensation, and a direction to take steps the ombudsman considers just and appropriate, "whether or not a court could order those steps" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=602]], s. 229(2)). A money award may cover financial loss, or other loss or damage of a kind specified in compulsory jurisdiction rules ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=602]], s. 229(3); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=603]], s. 229(11)).
- **The limit.** A money award may not exceed the monetary limit, which is "such amount as may be specified" in compulsory jurisdiction rules. Above it the ombudsman may only recommend that the respondent pays the balance ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=602]], s. 229(5)–(6); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=603]], s. 229(7), (11)). The Act sets no figure.
- **Enforcement.** A money award may carry interest, and the complainant can enforce it under Part III of Schedule 17. A direction can be enforced by injunction, or in Scotland by an order of the Court of Session, and only the complainant may bring those proceedings ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=603]], s. 229(8)–(10)).
- **Costs.** The scheme operator's costs rules, made with FCA approval, may let an ombudsman award costs. They can't make the complainant pay the respondent's costs. They can make the complainant contribute to the scheme operator's costs where the complainant's conduct was improper or unreasonable, or caused unreasonable delay ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=604]], s. 230(1)–(4)).
- **Published determinations.** The scheme operator must publish a report of every determination, unless the ombudsman thinks that inappropriate. Without the complainant's consent, a report may not name them or include particulars likely to identify them ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=605]], s. 230A(1)–(3)).

### Information (ss. 231–233)
- **Power to require information.** By written notice, an ombudsman may require a party to a complaint to provide specified information or produce specified documents, within a reasonable period. The power covers only what the ombudsman considers necessary to determine the complaint ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=606]], s. 231(1)–(3)). The ombudsman may copy a document and require an explanation of it. If a document isn't produced, they may require the person to say where it is ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=606]], s. 231(4)–(5)).
- **Failure to comply.** The ombudsman may certify the failure to the High Court, or in Scotland the Court of Session. If the failure was without reasonable excuse, the court may deal with the defaulter, and with a body corporate's directors or other officers, as if in contempt ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=606]], s. 232(1); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=607]], s. 232(2)–(3)).
- **Duty to tell the FCA.** The scheme operator must disclose to the FCA information that it thinks would or might help the FCA advance its operational objectives, including the competitiveness and growth objective ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=607]], s. 232A(1)–(2)).
- **Data protection.** Section 233 inserts a subject-information exemption into s. 31 of the Data Protection Act 1998, for personal data processed for the scheme's functions ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=608]], s. 233).

### Funding and successors (ss. 234–234B)
- **Industry funding.** To fund setting up the scheme and running its compulsory jurisdiction, the FCA may make rules requiring authorised persons, e-money issuers and payment service providers to pay specified amounts to it or to the scheme operator ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=608]], s. 234(1)–(2)).
- **Funding by consumer credit licensees: omitted.** Section 234A was omitted with effect from 1 April 2014 and is not current law ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=609]], s. 234A, F1775).
- **Transfers of liability.** Where a successor takes on a liability of the firm that would have been the respondent, the complaint may be dealt with as if the successor were the respondent ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=609]], s. 234B(1)–(2)).

## Conflicts and open points
- **Prospective text.** None in pp. 596–609.
- **Omitted sections.** Sections 226A (consumer credit jurisdiction) and 234A (funding by consumer credit licensees) are omitted, and so are ss. 229(4A) and 229(12) ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=603]], F1757, F1760). None of them is current law.
- **Agrees with DISP 3.7.** The Act leaves the award limit to rules. The figure is in DISP 3.7 ([[raw/fca-disp-3-7-ombudsman-awards.md]]). The Act also agrees with DISP 3.7 on directions a court couldn't order, on recommending the balance above the limit, and on enforcement. No conflict.
- **Section 233** amends the Data Protection Act 1998. The PDF gives no annotation on its current effect, and nothing in `raw/` covers it.
- **Schedule 17**, on the scheme operator and the enforcement of money awards (Part III), sits outside this Part and is `not compiled` on the map.
