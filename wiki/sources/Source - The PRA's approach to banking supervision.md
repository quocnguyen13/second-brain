# Source - The PRA's approach to banking supervision

**Raw file:** [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf]] · **Author or publisher:** Bank of England, Prudential Regulation Authority · **Published:** July 2023 (foreword by Sam Woods, PRA CEO); this issue revises the approach documents first published in 2012 · **Trust:** primary (a regulator's own publication) · **Ingested:** 2026-10-06

Page citations use the PDF's page numbers, which run 2 ahead of the printed ones (printed page 1 is PDF page 3).

## Summary
The PRA's standing statement of how it supervises deposit-takers and designated investment firms (a second document covers insurers). It explains its objectives, its three supervisory principles, how it assesses risk (potential impact plus a risk element framework), what it expects on governance, capital, liquidity, operational resilience and resolvability, and the tools it uses: the [[wiki/concepts/Proactive Intervention Framework]], periodic summary meetings, skilled persons reports, formal powers and enforcement. It also says how a new bank is authorised and how the PRA works with the FCA ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=7]]). The document also meets the PRA's statutory duty to publish guidance on how it advances its objectives ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=7]]).

## Key claims

### Who the PRA is and what it is for
- **Formed April 2013** as the UK's prudential regulator of deposit-takers, insurers and major investment firms, after the global financial crisis ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=5]]). It is part of the Bank of England ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=7]]).
- **Objectives.** Two primary objectives: safety and soundness of the firms it regulates, and, for insurers, policyholder protection. Two secondary objectives: effective competition, and (since the 2023 Act) international competitiveness and growth. The secondary objectives apply when it makes policies, codes and rules ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=8]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=9]]). See [[wiki/concepts/Regulatory objectives]].
- **Competition is weighed, not imposed.** The competition objective means the PRA should consider, but is not required to adopt, options that give greater benefits to competition ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=9]]).
- **Not a zero-failure regime.** The Act is explicit that it is not the PRA's role to ensure no firm fails; it works with the Bank as resolution authority so that failures are orderly ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=11]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=12]]). See [[wiki/concepts/Resolvability]].
- **Threshold Conditions and Fundamental Rules.** The [[wiki/concepts/Threshold Conditions]] are the minimum requirements a firm must meet at all times to keep its permission. The Fundamental Rules are high-level rules expressing the safety-and-soundness objective; breaching them may bear on the Threshold Conditions ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=12]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=13]]).
- **Regulatory principles.** Lists the principles it has regard to, and says the 2023 Act requires it to "have regard" to the UK's 2050 net-zero and environmental targets where relevant ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=13]]). See [[wiki/concepts/Regulatory principles]].

### Ring-fencing (emphasis)
- Safety and soundness includes a continued focus on ring-fencing to safeguard the continuity of **core services: retail deposits and related payment and overdraft services** ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=8]]).
- The general objective was amended in 2019 for ring-fenced bodies (RFBs). RFBs are supervised against more requirements than other banks; the PRA has group restructuring powers over an RFB's group, and gives an annual opinion on RFBs' compliance ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=54]]). See [[wiki/concepts/Ring-fencing]].

### How it supervises
- **Three principles:** judgement-based, forward-looking, focused on key risks; applied proportionately ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=14]]). Firms "should not … approach their relationship with us as a negotiation" ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=14]]).
- **Potential impact.** Every deposit-taker and designated investment firm is placed in one of four [[wiki/concepts/Potential impact categories]] (five until 2023). Categories are set by quantitative scoring then supervisory review, reviewed annually, and told to the firm ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=20]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=21]]).
- **Risk element framework.** Gross risk (potential impact, external context, business risk) overlaid with mitigating factors (management and governance, risk management and controls, capital, liquidity, operational resilience, resolvability) ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=18]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=19]]).
- **PIF (emphasis).** Each firm has a PIF stage, 1 to 5, reflecting its proximity to failure; the PRA does not routinely disclose it to the market or the firm ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=44]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=45]]). See [[wiki/concepts/Proactive Intervention Framework]].
- **Periodic Summary Meetings** set each firm's supervisory strategy; a letter follows, and the firm must allocate an SMF to each key risk ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=47]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=48]]).
- **Tools and powers.** Data validation, onsite reviews, s. 166 skilled persons reports (routinely used, no presumption of wrongdoing), Part 4A powers to vary a permission or impose requirements, and enforcement: financial penalties, public censure, prohibition ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=43]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=44]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=50]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=51]]).
- **Not a fraud regulator.** Onsite inspections are not designed to uncover all malpractice ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=49]]).

### Governance, capital and liquidity
- **Senior managers.** Under the [[wiki/concepts/Senior Managers and Certification Regime]], Candidates for SMFs that need the PRA's approval are approved only if both the PRA and the FCA are satisfied the person is fit and proper ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=23]]).
- **Capital.** Pillar 1 plus Pillar 2A is the minimum at all times; the combined buffer and a non-public PRA buffer sit on top; using the combined buffer restricts distributions ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=30]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=31]]). Leverage ratio minimum 3.25% ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=33]]). See [[wiki/concepts/Regulatory capital framework]].
- **Liquidity.** NSFR is the starting point for funding; LCR for the liquid-asset buffer; buffers are meant to be used in stress ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=34]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=35]]).
- **Operational resilience.** Firms set impact tolerances for important business services and must stay within them in severe but plausible scenarios ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=36]]).

### Authorising a new bank (emphasis)
- Deposit-taking needs PRA authorisation; the application is a joint PRA–FCA assessment, prudential and conduct. The firm is authorised only if both regulators are satisfied it will meet their Threshold Conditions, now and on an ongoing basis, including whether it could be resolved in an orderly way. The PRA leads a single administrative process and is the decision maker ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=42]]). See [[wiki/concepts/New bank authorisation]].

### Working with others
- **FCA.** The FCA is the conduct regulator for PRA-regulated firms. A statutory duty and an MoU govern co-ordination; each regulator focuses on its own objectives; they share information on dual-regulated firms and run domestic supervisory colleges. The PRA CEO sits on the FCA Board and the FCA CEO on the PRC ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=16]]). See [[wiki/concepts/FCA-PRA coordination]].
- **Bank of England.** The [[wiki/entities/Financial Policy Committee]] can make recommendations and give directions to the PRA ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=15]]). The [[wiki/entities/Prudential Regulation Committee]] makes the PRA's most important decisions ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=48]]).
- **FSCS.** The [[wiki/entities/Financial Services Compensation Scheme]] is the compensation fund of last resort ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=16]]).
