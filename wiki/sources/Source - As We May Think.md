# As We May Think

**Raw file:** [[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf]] · **Author or publisher:** Vannevar Bush, in *The Atlantic Monthly* · **Published:** July 1945 (clip source: https://web.mit.edu/sts.035/www/PDFs/think.pdf) · **Ingested:** 2026-09-22

## Summary
Bush, then Director of the U.S. Office of Scientific Research and Development, argues that scientific record-keeping has fallen behind the pace of research: too much gets published for any specialist to track, and conventional indexing — filing each item in one fixed place — can't keep up. He surveys near-future mechanization (microfilm compression, dry photography, speech-to-text, faster calculating machines) and proposes the "memex," a personal device for storing a lifetime of books and records and linking them by association rather than classification, producing reusable, shareable "trails" through the material ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf]]).

## Key claims
- The volume of research has outrun the "generations old" methods used to transmit and review it; significant findings get lost in the mass of the inconsequential, as Mendel's genetics once was ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=3]]).
- Near-future mechanization — faster photography, dry (instant-development) photography, microfilm at up to 100:1 linear compression, speech-to-text via a combined Vocoder/stenotype, and electrical calculating machines up to 100x faster than then-current ones — will change what's possible for the scientific record, without needing unknown future breakthroughs ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf]]).
- Conventional indexing is artificial: an item sits in one place under one classification path, while "the human mind operates by association," jumping along "an intricate web of trails" ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=14]]).
- Proposes the [[wiki/concepts/Memex]], built on [[wiki/concepts/Associative indexing]], letting a user permanently link any two items and later replay, branch, annotate, and share the resulting trail ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf]]).
- Anticipates "wholly new forms of encyclopedias," pre-built with associative trails, and a new profession of "trail blazers" who build useful paths through the common record for others to reuse ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=17]]).
- Closes by speculating — explicitly labelled "a doubly involved guess," not a prediction — about bypassing the senses and recording nerve impulses directly ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=18]]).
