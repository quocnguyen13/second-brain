# Source - Wikipedia on the FCA

**Raw file:** [[raw/wikipedia-financial-conduct-authority.md]] · **Author or publisher:** Wikipedia contributors · **Published:** article created 2010-06-16; retrieved 2026-09-30 · **Trust:** commentary (encyclopedia) · **Ingested:** 2026-10-06

## Summary
The English Wikipedia article on the FCA: what it is, its history from 2013, its powers, the sectors it covers, the Payment Systems Regulator it set up, its complaints body, its leaders and the criticism it has faced ([[raw/wikipedia-financial-conduct-authority.md]] · commentary). It is the only source in the wiki on the FCA's history after 2013, its payments work and its critics. Its headline firm figure rests on a 2010 news source and conflicts with the FCA's own figure.

## Key claims

### Status and scale
- Independent of government, funded by fees from the industry, structured like the FSA as a company limited by guarantee ([[raw/wikipedia-financial-conduct-authority.md]] · commentary).
- Responsible for the conduct of "around 58,000 businesses" employing 2.2 million people. The cited source is a June 2010 Businessweek article ([[raw/wikipedia-financial-conduct-authority.md]] · commentary). See Conflicts below.

### History
- The Financial Services Act 2012 came into force on 1 April 2013, "abolished" the FSA, gave the Bank of England responsibility for financial stability and created the FPC, PRA and FCA ([[raw/wikipedia-financial-conduct-authority.md]] · commentary). See Conflicts below.
- **Payments (product lens).** On 26 October 2015 the FCA banned premium-rate 084, 087 and 09 numbers for customer contact. From 14 September 2019 it introduced [[wiki/concepts/Strong customer authentication]] rules under PSD2 ([[raw/wikipedia-financial-conduct-authority.md]] · commentary).
- **Consumer credit (product lens).** The FCA has regulated consumer credit since 1 April 2014, taking over from the Office of Fair Trading ([[raw/wikipedia-financial-conduct-authority.md]] · commentary).
- **Payment Systems Regulator.** Created by the FCA in April 2015 under s. 40 of the Financial Services (Banking Reform) Act 2013 ([[raw/wikipedia-financial-conduct-authority.md]] · commentary). See [[wiki/entities/Payment Systems Regulator]].
- **AML.** OPBAS, set up in January 2018 within the FCA, oversees 22 accountancy and legal professional-body AML supervisors. The FCA is the AML/CTF supervisor for cryptoasset businesses, which must register before operating ([[raw/wikipedia-financial-conduct-authority.md]] · commentary).
- **Listing Rules** overhauled from 29 July 2024 into a single listing category ([[raw/wikipedia-financial-conduct-authority.md]] · commentary).

### Powers
- Can set minimum standards and requirements on products, ban products for up to a year while considering an indefinite ban, order misleading promotions withdrawn and publish that, investigate, and freeze assets of those under investigation ([[raw/wikipedia-financial-conduct-authority.md]] · commentary).
- Example: in April 2023 it ordered WealthTek LLP to cease operations over a potential £81.4 million client-asset shortfall, obtained a worldwide freezing order against its principal partner, John Dance, and in December 2024 charged him with fraud and other offences ([[raw/wikipedia-financial-conduct-authority.md]] · commentary).

### Leadership and accountability
- Chief executives: Martin Wheatley (2013–2015), Tracey McDermott (interim), Andrew Bailey (2016–2020), Christopher Woolard (interim), Nikhil Rathi (from 1 October 2020, reappointed in April 2025 to 2030). Chairs: John Griffith-Jones, Charles Randell, Richard Lloyd (interim), Ashley Alder (from 2023) ([[raw/wikipedia-financial-conduct-authority.md]] · commentary).
- The Financial Regulators Complaints Commissioner handles complaints against the FCA independently ([[raw/wikipedia-financial-conduct-authority.md]] · commentary).

### Criticism
- The Parliamentary Commission on Banking Standards (2013) called the FSA and FCA response to interest rate swap mis-selling "inadequate"; the Treasury Committee chair spoke of an "extraordinary blunder" over the 2014 business-plan press briefing; an APPG report in 2024 called the FCA "incompetent at best, dishonest at worst", followed by a supplementary report in 2025 ([[raw/wikipedia-financial-conduct-authority.md]] · commentary).
