# Source - DISP 2.8 Referral time limits

**Raw file:** [[raw/fca-disp-2-8-referral-time-limits.md]] · **Author or publisher:** Financial Conduct Authority, FCA Handbook (DISP 2.8, "Was the complaint referred to the Financial Ombudsman Service in time?") · **Published:** last updated 2018-01-13; viewed as of 2026-10-14, the clip date · **Trust:** primary (a regulator's own rules) · **Ingested:** 2026-10-14

## Summary
The FCA Handbook rules that decide when the [[wiki/entities/Financial Ombudsman Service]] can take a complaint. The firm must have had its chance first. Then the complainant has six months from the firm's final response. There is also an outer limit of six years from the event, or three years from when the complainant knew of the problem. The section also has special rules for the pensions review, mortgage endowments and PPI ([[raw/fca-disp-2-8-referral-time-limits.md]]). See [[wiki/concepts/Ombudsman referral time limits]].

## Key claims
- **The firm goes first.** The Ombudsman can consider a complaint only when one of these holds ([[raw/fca-disp-2-8-referral-time-limits.md]], DISP 2.8.1R):
  - the firm has sent its final response or summary resolution communication;
  - for a complaint that is not a payment-services or e-money complaint, eight weeks have passed since the firm received it;
  - for a payment-services or e-money complaint, 15 business days have passed with no holding response, or 35 business days where a holding response was sent;
  - for a complaint under a consumer redress scheme, the firm has sent its redress determination or missed the scheme's deadline.

  It can take a complaint earlier if the firm consents, the Ombudsman has told the complainant the firm's deadline and that the firm may be quicker, and the complainant still wants the Ombudsman to deal with it.
- **A whole complaint on the short clock.** If a firm chooses to treat a whole complaint under the payment-services deadlines, DISP 2.8 applies as if the whole complaint were a payment-services or e-money complaint ([[raw/fca-disp-2-8-referral-time-limits.md]], General time limits).
- **Outer limits.** The Ombudsman cannot consider a complaint referred to it ([[raw/fca-disp-2-8-referral-time-limits.md]], DISP 2.8.2R):
  - more than six months after the firm sent its final response, redress determination or summary resolution communication; or
  - more than six years after the event, or, if later, three years after the complainant became aware, or ought reasonably to have become aware, of cause for complaint. This limit doesn't apply if the complainant complained within it and has a written acknowledgement or other record.
- **Exceptions to the outer limits.** The limits do not apply where ([[raw/fca-disp-2-8-referral-time-limits.md]], DISP 2.8.2R(3)–(5)):
  - in the Ombudsman's view, exceptional circumstances caused the delay;
  - the Ombudsman Transitional Order requires it;
  - the firm has consented. This option is not available for a "relevant complaint" under s. 404B(3) of the Act.
- **Consent is final.** A firm that consents under DISP 2.8.2R(5) may not withdraw its consent ([[raw/fca-disp-2-8-referral-time-limits.md]]).
- **What starts the six months.** Only a final response, redress determination or summary resolution communication starts it, and that response must tell the complainant about the six-month limit ([[raw/fca-disp-2-8-referral-time-limits.md]]).
- **Exceptional circumstances.** The rules' one example is a complainant who has been or is incapacitated ([[raw/fca-disp-2-8-referral-time-limits.md]]).
- **Special cases** ([[raw/fca-disp-2-8-referral-time-limits.md]]):
  - **Pensions review and FSAVC review.** The six-year and three-year limits don't apply.
  - **Mortgage endowments.** A "high risk" letter starts a three-year clock, but only if the complainant is also told, at least six months before the end, when time will run out. A letter saying only that there is "a risk" does not start the clock.
  - **PPI.** A complaint about a payment protection sale made on or before 29 August 2017 had to be made by 29 August 2019. The exceptions are exceptional circumstances, the firm's consent, and certain later claim rejections.
