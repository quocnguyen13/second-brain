# Source - FCA glossary small business

**Raw file:** [[raw/fca-glossary-small-business.md]] · **Author or publisher:** Financial Conduct Authority, FCA Handbook Glossary ("small business", G1095) · **Published:** undated; retrieved 2026-10-14 · **Trust:** primary (a regulator's own rules) · **Ingested:** 2026-10-14

## Summary
The FCA Handbook Glossary defines "small business" in two ways, depending on where the term is used. The DISP meaning sets the test for small-business complainants to the Financial Ombudsman Service ([[raw/fca-glossary-small-business.md]]). See [[wiki/concepts/Eligible complainant]].

## Key claims
- **In DISP.** An enterprise that ([[raw/fca-glossary-small-business.md]], (2)):
  - is not a micro-enterprise;
  - has annual turnover under £6.5 million;
  - **and** either employs fewer than 50 persons **or** has a balance sheet total under £5 million.

  Articles 3 to 6 of the Annex to the Micro-enterprise Recommendation must be applied in deciding this. The same meaning applies in the definition of "larger commercial customer".
- **In COMP.** For the compensation rules, and in the definition of "relevant credit union client", a small business is a partnership, body corporate, unincorporated association or mutual association with annual turnover under £1 million ([[raw/fca-glossary-small-business.md]], (1)).

## Conflicts and open points
- **Two meanings.** The term means different things in DISP and in COMP, and the meanings don't conflict. For a complaint, the DISP meaning applies.
- **The DISP meaning.** It sits above the micro-enterprise tier, so a business is either a micro-enterprise or a small business, never both.
