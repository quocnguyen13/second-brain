# Source - Claude on who regulates what (2026-09-27)

**Raw file:** [[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · **Author or publisher:** Claude (a study note for your UK banking foundations plan, sub-topic 1.1) · **Published:** 2026-09-27 · **Trust:** ai (Claude output) · **Ingested:** 2026-10-14

## Summary
A Claude study note mapping who regulates what in UK banking. It covers HM Treasury, the Bank of England's three committees (MPC, FPC, PRA/PRC) and the FCA; the split between dual-regulated and solo-regulated firms; how FSMA makes the two regulators coordinate; a Vietnam ↔ UK comparison; a case study of TSB's 2018 IT migration, which brought fines from both regulators; and the Financial Services and Markets Bill [HL] 2026–27. Its claims were checked one by one against `raw/`. Those a raw file states cite that file. The rest are marked `· AI`: they are leads, not facts, until a source in `raw/` backs them.

## Key claims

### Backed by raw/
All of these were already on the wiki's pages, so this note added none of them.
- The PRA is the Bank of England acting through the Prudential Regulation Committee ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2A).
- The PRA's general objective is safety and soundness. Its secondary objectives are competition, and competitiveness and growth ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 2B, 2H).
- The FCA's objectives are well-functioning markets, consumer protection, integrity and competition, plus the growth objective ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 1B–1E, 1EB).
- The FCA's growth objective dates from 2023 ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- The FCA began operating on 1 April 2013, taking over conduct and relevant prudential regulation from the FSA ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]). The PRA was formed in April 2013, after the global financial crisis ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=5]]).
- The FCA regulates the conduct of tens of thousands of firms: about 35,500 ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- The Treasury can send the FCA recommendations ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1JA).
- FSMA 2023 began moving assimilated EU law into the regulators' rulebooks ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=25]]).
- **Dual vs solo.** Deposit-takers, insurers and designated investment firms are prudentially regulated by the PRA and conduct-regulated by the FCA. The FCA prudentially supervises firms that are not PRA-regulated ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=3]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=4]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=6]]). The regulators share information and coordinate ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=7]]).
- **Coordination.** FSMA sets a duty to coordinate (s. 3D), requires a memorandum of understanding (s. 3E) and gives the PRA a power to direct the FCA not to act (s. 3I) ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]]). The PRA needs the FCA's consent to grant a permission ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=21]]).
- The Bank pursues monetary policy and financial stability mainly through the MPC and the FPC ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=1]]). The FPC acts on risks to the system as a whole ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=15]]). The PRC is accountable to Parliament, like the MPC and the FPC ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=48]]).
- The PRA expects firms to manage operational as well as financial risks ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=36]]). The note says this in the context of TSB; the passage says it in general.
- The FCA describes itself as an independent public body ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).

### Unbacked (AI)
**The map**
- UK financial regulation is divided by purpose, not by type of firm ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- HM Treasury decides which activities need authorisation: the perimeter ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- HM Treasury lists the regulated activities in secondary legislation ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- HM Treasury formally tells the PRA, as well as the FCA, what to weigh ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI). Section 1JA in `raw/` covers only the FCA.
- FSMA s. 19 sets the "general prohibition" on carrying on a regulated activity without being authorised or exempt ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- FSMA s. 23 makes breaching the general prohibition an offence ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- FSMA ss. 55E and 55F set separate FCA and PRA routes for giving permission ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- The 2007–08 crisis showed that no single body clearly owned financial stability ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- The [[wiki/entities/Monetary Policy Committee]] sets Bank Rate ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- The MPC sets Bank Rate to meet the government's 2% inflation target ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- The FPC's tools include a cap on the share of new mortgages at high loan-to-income multiples ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- HM Treasury legislated to bring interest-free buy-now-pay-later into regulation ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- The FCA's BNPL rules have applied since 15 July 2026 ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).

**Dual vs solo (emphasis)**
- E-money institutions, payment institutions, non-bank lenders and brokers are solo-regulated by the FCA ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- Monzo and Starling, as banks, must meet PRA capital and liquidity requirements and FCA conduct standards ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- Wise's UK e-money business deals only with the FCA ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- A bank can lend out deposits and offer FSCS protection ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- An e-money institution must safeguard customer money and cannot lend it ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).

**Working with two regulators**
- A product that needs a regulated activity the firm has no permission for needs a variation of permission ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- A variation of permission can take months ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- FCA Principle 11 requires firms to tell the FCA anything it would reasonably expect to know ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- PRA Fundamental Rule 7 places the same duty towards the PRA ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- That duty can cover a significant new product or a risky change programme ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).

**Vietnam ↔ UK** (kept on this page only, as you asked)
- The State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) is a ministerial-level agency of the Government ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- The SBV acts as the central bank and carries out state management of monetary, banking and foreign-exchange activity ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- The SBV's aims cover currency stability, safe and sound banks, and a safe, efficient national payment system ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- In Vietnam, the SBV covers licensing, prudential ratios, lending rules and caps on short-term deposit rates ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- For years, the SBV set each bank's annual credit growth limit ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- UK regulators are operationally independent ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI). Backed in part: the FCA calls itself an independent public body ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- HM Treasury steers the regulators through legislation, recommendation letters and appointments, not day-to-day direction ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI). Backed in part: Treasury recommendations to the FCA ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1JA), and the Chancellor's appointments to the PRC ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=48]]).
- The UK has no credit growth quotas ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
- The UK has no rate caps on mainstream products; high-cost short-term credit is the exception ([[raw/claude-2026-09-27-foundations-1-1-who-regulates-what.md]] · AI).
