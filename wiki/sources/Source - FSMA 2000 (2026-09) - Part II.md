# Source - FSMA 2000 (2026-09) - Part II

**Raw file:** [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf]] · **Author or publisher:** UK Parliament, via legislation.gov.uk · **Published:** Act of 14 June 2000; revised text up to date to 10 September 2026 · **Trust:** primary (legislation) · **Ingested:** 2026-10-17
**Part of:** [[wiki/sources/Source - FSMA 2000 (2026-09)]] · **Pages:** 51–70 · **Sections:** 19–30

## Summary
Part II, "Regulated and Prohibited Activities", draws the perimeter of UK financial regulation. Section 19 forbids anyone to carry on a regulated activity in the UK unless they are authorised or exempt ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=51]], s. 19(1)). Section 22 makes "regulated activity" whatever the Treasury specifies by order ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=56]], s. 22(1); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=57]], s. 22(5)). Section 21 puts a parallel restriction on financial promotion ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=53]], s. 21(1)). The rest of the Part sets the consequences: criminal offences (ss. 23–25), and agreements that can't be enforced against the customer (ss. 26–30), with a separate regime for credit in which the FCA, not the court, decides whether a tainted agreement may still be enforced ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=60]], s. 23; [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=62]], ss. 24–25; [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=63]], s. 26; [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=64]], s. 27; [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=69]], s. 30; [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=67]], s. 28A(3)).

## Key claims

### The general prohibition (s. 19)
- **The rule.** No person may carry on a regulated activity in the United Kingdom, or "purport to do so", unless they are an authorised person or an exempt person ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=51]], s. 19(1)). The Act calls this "the general prohibition" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=51]], s. 19(2)). See [[wiki/concepts/General prohibition]].

### Acting outside a permission (s. 20)
- **Authorised, but not for this activity.** An authorised person that carries on a regulated activity in the UK otherwise than under its permission (under Part 4A or any other provision of the Act) is treated as breaching a requirement imposed by the FCA. For a PRA-authorised person, the breach is of a requirement imposed by both the FCA and the PRA ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=52]], s. 20(1), (1A)).
- **What follows, and what doesn't.** Such a breach is not an offence, except under s. 23(1A); doesn't make a transaction void or unenforceable, except under s. 26A; and gives no right of action for breach of statutory duty, except in prescribed cases, where a person who suffers loss can sue ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=52]], s. 20(2)–(3)). Sections 23(1A) and 26A single out credit (see below).
- **Product-owner reading.** Launching a product that needs a regulated activity outside the firm's permission is a breach of a regulator's requirement under s. 20, not of the general prohibition, which binds only persons who are neither authorised nor exempt ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=51]], s. 19(1); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=52]], s. 20(1)).

### Financial promotion (s. 21) (emphasis)
- **The restriction.** A person must not, in the course of business, communicate an invitation or inducement to engage in investment activity, or to engage in claims management activity ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=53]], s. 21(1); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=54]], s. 21(1)(b)). The claims management limb extends to England, Wales and Scotland ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=55]], F135). See [[wiki/concepts/Financial promotion]].
- **Two ways through.** The restriction doesn't apply if the communicator is an authorised person, or if an authorised person approves the content of the communication ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=54]], s. 21(2)).
- **Approving others' promotions is gated.** An authorised person may approve a communication only if the approval is permitted under s. 55NA, "which enables approval to be given with FCA permission", or falls within an exemption in regulations under s. 55NB ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=54]], s. 21(2A)). This gateway was inserted by the Financial Services and Markets Act 2023 and has been fully in force since 7 February 2024 ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=55]], F136).
- **Reach.** A communication from outside the UK is caught only if it is capable of having an effect in the UK ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=54]], s. 21(3)). "Communicate" includes causing a communication to be made ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=55]], s. 21(13)).
- **Treasury sets the detail.** The Treasury may specify by order when a person is or isn't acting in the course of business, and circumstances in which the restriction doesn't apply, which may include compliance with financial promotion rules ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=54]], s. 21(4)–(6)).
- **What counts as investment activity.** Entering or offering to enter an agreement whose making or performance is a "controlled activity", or exercising rights under a "controlled investment"; both are of kinds specified by Treasury order ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=54]], s. 21(8)–(10); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=55]], s. 21(15)). Schedule 2 (except para. 26) applies to define them ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=55]], s. 21(11)).
- **Cryptoassets.** "Investment" includes any asset, right or interest, including a cryptoasset ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=55]], s. 21(14)), words inserted from 29 August 2023 ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=55]], F139). Section 21 is also modified, for specified purposes from 25 February 2026, by the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026 ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=55]], C78).

### Regulated activities (ss. 22–22B)
- **The definition.** An activity is a regulated activity if it is of a specified kind, carried on by way of business, and relates to an investment of a specified kind, or, for activities specified for this purpose, to property of any kind ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=56]], s. 22(1)). "Specified" means specified in an order made by the Treasury ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=57]], s. 22(5)).
- **Beyond investments.** Also regulated: specified activities relating to information about a person's financial standing, or to administering a benchmark ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=56]], s. 22(1A)), and specified claims management services carried on in Great Britain ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=56]], s. 22(1B)).
- **Schedule 2** supplements s. 22 but does not limit the Treasury's powers ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=57]], s. 22(2)–(3)). "Investment" includes a cryptoasset ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=57]], s. 22(4)).
- **PRA-regulated activities.** The Treasury may by order specify which regulated activities are "PRA-regulated activities" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=59]], s. 22A(1)). The section names none itself. See [[wiki/concepts/Solo and dual regulation]].
- **Parliament's say.** The first such order, and any later one that makes an activity PRA-regulated or takes one out, or amends primary legislation, needs a draft approved by both Houses. In an urgent case it can be made first, and lapses after 28 days unless both Houses approve it ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=59]], s. 22B(1)–(5); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=60]], s. 22B(6)).

### Offences (ss. 23–25)
- **Breaching the general prohibition is a crime.** On summary conviction, up to six months' imprisonment or a fine up to the statutory maximum, or both; on indictment, up to two years or a fine, or both ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=60]], s. 23(1)). It is "an authorisation offence" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=61]], s. 23(2)). The accused has a defence if they took all reasonable precautions and exercised all due diligence ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=61]], s. 23(3)).
- **Credit is the exception for authorised firms (emphasis).** An authorised person commits an offence if it carries on a "credit-related regulated activity" in the UK otherwise than under its permission ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=60]], s. 23(1A)). For other activities, acting outside permission is not an offence ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=52]], s. 20(2)(a)).
  - **What counts as credit-related.** A regulated activity the Treasury designates by order. It may designate only activities that involve entering into or administering an agreement under which the person provides credit, exercising a lender's rights under such an agreement, or taking steps to collect debts due under one ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=60]], s. 23(1B)–(1C)).
  - **Secured on land is out.** No activity may be designated if the borrower's obligation is secured on land ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=60]], s. 23(1D)). "Credit" includes any cash loan or other financial accommodation ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=60]], s. 23(1E)).
  - **Penalty.** On indictment, up to two years or a fine, or both; on summary conviction, a term up to the general limit in a magistrates' court in England and Wales, 12 months in Scotland, six months in Northern Ireland ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=60]], s. 23(1F); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=61]], s. 23(1G)).
  - **No double jeopardy with the regulator.** No prosecution under s. 23(1A) where either regulator has already acted under s. 205, 206 or 206A for the same contravention ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=61]], s. 23(5)).
  - **Parliament's say.** The first designation order, and any later one that makes an activity credit-related, needs a draft approved by both Houses ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=61]], s. 23A).
- **False claims.** A person neither authorised nor exempt commits an offence by describing themselves as authorised or exempt, or by holding themselves out in a way likely to be understood so. Summary only: up to six months or a level 5 fine, or both; due-diligence defence ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=62]], s. 24(1)–(3)).
- **Breaching s. 21 is a crime (emphasis).** Contravening the financial promotion restriction carries the same penalties as breaching the general prohibition: up to two years on indictment ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=62]], s. 25(1)). Defences: a reasonable belief that the content was prepared, or approved in line with s. 21(2A), by an authorised person; or all reasonable precautions and due diligence ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=62]], s. 25(2)).

### Enforceability of agreements (ss. 26–30)
- **Unauthorised business: unenforceable against the customer.** An agreement made in the course of a regulated activity carried on in breach of the general prohibition is unenforceable against the other party, who can recover money or property paid and compensation for loss ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=63]], s. 26(1)–(2)).
- **Through an unauthorised intermediary.** An agreement an authorised provider makes in consequence of something a third party said or did while carrying on a regulated activity in breach of the general prohibition, or a credit-related regulated activity in breach of s. 20, is also unenforceable against the customer ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=64]], s. 27(1); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=65]], s. 27(1)(c)–(d), (1A)–(2)). For a regulated credit agreement or regulated consumer hire agreement, this applies only if the provider knew, before the agreement was made, that the third party was involved in making it or in preparing for it ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=65]], s. 27(1ZA), (5)).
- **Deposits are different.** Sections 26 and 27 don't apply to accepting deposits ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=63]], s. 26(4); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=65]], s. 27(4)). Instead a depositor who can't recover money without delay may ask the court to order an unauthorised deposit-taker to return it; the court may refuse if return would not be just and equitable, having regard to whether the deposit-taker reasonably believed it wasn't breaching the general prohibition ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=68]], s. 29(1)–(4)).
- **Non-credit cases: the court decides.** For agreements unenforceable under s. 26 or 27, other than credit-related ones, compensation is as agreed or set by the court, and the court may let the agreement be enforced, or money be kept, if just and equitable. It looks at whether the person reasonably believed they weren't breaching the general prohibition (s. 26), or whether the provider knew the third party was (s. 27) ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=66]], s. 28(1)–(6)). An authorisation offence makes an agreement illegal or invalid only to the extent ss. 26–27 provide ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=66]], s. 28(9)).

### The credit-agreement rules (ss. 26A, 28A, 28B) (emphasis)
- **Credit outside permission: unenforceable.** An agreement an authorised person makes in breach of s. 20, in the course of a credit-related regulated activity that involves entering into or administering a credit agreement (s. 23(1C)(a)), is unenforceable against the other party. They can recover money or property paid and compensation for loss ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=63]], s. 26A(1)–(2)). This is the exception that s. 20(2)(b) points to: for other activities, acting outside permission leaves transactions enforceable ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=52]], s. 20(2)(b)).
- **Administering and collecting need cover too.** Where administering an agreement is a credit-related regulated activity, whoever holds the lender's rights may enforce the agreement only if they have permission for that activity, are an appointed representative or exempt person for it, or fall under Part 20 ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=64]], s. 26A(4)). The same applies to taking steps to collect debts due under the agreement ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=64]], s. 26A(5)).
- **The FCA, not the court, decides on credit.** For a credit-related agreement unenforceable under s. 26, 26A or 27, compensation is as agreed or as the FCA specifies by written notice ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=67]], s. 28A(1)–(2)). On the relevant firm's application, the FCA may allow the agreement to be enforced, or money kept, if just and equitable ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=67]], s. 28A(3)).
  - It must weigh whether the firm reasonably believed it was breaching neither the general prohibition nor s. 20 (ss. 26, 26A cases), or whether the provider knew the third party was breaching one of them (s. 27 cases) ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=67]], s. 28A(4)–(6)).
  - An application may cover specified agreements or a description of them, such as a book of loans made at a specified time. The FCA may limit its determination, or make it conditional on the firm doing specified things ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=67]], s. 28A(7), (9)).
- **Procedure and appeal.** The FCA's notice must give reasons and explain the right to refer the matter to the Tribunal. It must copy the notice, so far as reasonably practicable, to others affected. An aggrieved person may refer the determination to the Tribunal ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=68]], s. 28B).
- **Product-owner reading.** For a lender, a gap in permission costs more than a breach finding: the loans can't be enforced against borrowers until the FCA allows it (ss. 26A, 28A), and the firm may face prosecution unless a regulator has already acted (s. 23(1A), (5)) ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=63]], s. 26A(1); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=67]], s. 28A(3); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=60]], s. 23(1A); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=61]], s. 23(5)).

### Agreements after an unlawful promotion (s. 30) (emphasis)
- **Unenforceable against the customer.** A customer who enters a controlled agreement, or exercises rights under a controlled investment, in consequence of a communication that breached s. 21(1) is not bound by it, and can recover money or property paid and compensation for loss ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=69]], s. 30(1)–(3)).
- **The court may relent** if just and equitable. It weighs whether the applicant, if they made the communication, reasonably believed it wasn't unlawful; or, if they didn't, whether they knew the agreement followed from one ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=69]], s. 30(4)–(7)).
