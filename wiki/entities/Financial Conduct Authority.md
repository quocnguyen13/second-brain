# Financial Conduct Authority

The FCA is the UK regulator for all authorised persons ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1L(1)).

## What the sources say
- **Origins.** In law, the FCA is the body formerly called the Financial Services Authority, renamed ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1A(1)). It began operating as the FCA on 1 April 2013, taking over conduct and relevant prudential regulation from the FSA ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- **Scale.** The FCA gives these approximate figures, last updated on 2026-07-09 after its Annual Report ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]):
  - it regulates the **conduct** of about 35,500 firms
  - it **prudentially supervises** about 34,000 firms
  - about 14,000 firms are **subject to the prudential standards in its Handbook**

  The page doesn't explain how the last two figures relate.
- **How it regulates.** The FCA's own account ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]):
  - **Authorise.** Firms and individuals must be authorised or registered by the FCA to carry on regulated activities. A firm must show it meets a range of requirements first.
  - **Supervise.** It then supervises the firm to check that it keeps meeting its standards and rules. Supervision is proportionate: it gives priority to the firms and areas that pose a higher risk to its objectives, by size, complexity and potential impact on consumers. It uses data to act faster against firms and individuals likely to cause harm.
  - **Enforce.** Where standards aren't met, it uses a range of enforcement powers, including criminal prosecutions for financial crime.
  - **Rules and market studies.** It also makes rules, issues guidance and standards, and uses market studies to find and remedy harm across a market.
- **Status and funding.** It is an independent public body "funded entirely by the fees we charge regulated firms", and it is accountable to the Treasury and to Parliament ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- **Self-assessed value.** The FCA estimates that it adds at least £14 of benefit to society for every £1 of running costs ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- **Objectives.** Its strategic objective is that the relevant markets function well. Its operational objectives are consumer protection, integrity and competition. In its general functions it must act compatibly with the strategic objective and advance at least one operational objective ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1B(1)–(3)). The FCA's own page lists the same objectives ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]). See [[wiki/concepts/Regulatory objectives]].
- **Competition duty.** It must promote effective competition in the interests of consumers, as far as that is compatible with the consumer protection and integrity objectives ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1B(4)).
- **Secondary objective.** Since 29 August 2023 it has competitiveness and growth: facilitating the UK's international competitiveness and its medium-to-long-term growth, "subject to aligning with relevant international standards" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 1B(4A), 1EB; [[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- **Relevant markets.** These are the financial markets, the markets for regulated financial services, and services provided without breaching the general prohibition by persons who are not authorised ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1F).
- **Supervision.** It supervises every authorised person, not only those the PRA leaves out ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1L(1)). It also monitors and enforces against certain persons who are not authorised ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1L(2)–(3)).
- **General functions.** These are making rules, making technical standards, issuing codes, giving general guidance, and setting general policy ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1B(6)).
- **Other duties.** It must have regard to the [[wiki/concepts/Regulatory principles]] and to minimising the use of firms for financial crime ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1B(5)).
- **Panels.** It must consult practitioners and consumers through statutory panels: Practitioner, Smaller Business Practitioner, Markets Practitioner, Consumer, and Listing Authority Advisory ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 1M–1QA). It must consider their representations and publish responses ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1R).
- **Public consultations.** Each consultation must disclose its engagement with the statutory panels of the FCA, the PRA or the Payment Systems Regulator ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1RB).
- **Treasury accountability.** The FCA must answer Treasury recommendations within 12 months ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1JA). It can be subject to independent reviews of its economy, efficiency and effectiveness, which may not question the merits of its general policy ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1S).
- **Ring-fencing without the PRA.** If a core activity is ever not PRA-regulated, the FCA takes on a continuity objective for ring-fenced bodies ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1IA).
- **Bank of England.** It must co-operate with the Bank on the Bank's Financial Stability Objective ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3Q).
- **The PRA's veto.** It can be directed by the PRA not to use its powers where stability is at risk. It need not comply if compliance would breach an international obligation of the UK ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3I(8)).

## Where sources disagree
