# Financial Ombudsman Service

The Financial Ombudsman Service is the body that considers complaints against financial firms once the firm has had its chance to resolve them, within set time limits ([[raw/fca-disp-2-8-referral-time-limits.md]], DISP 2.8.1R, 2.8.2R).

## What the sources say
- **Statutory basis.** FSMA Part XVI provides for a scheme under which certain disputes are resolved "quickly and with minimum formality by an independent person". A body corporate, the scheme operator, administers it, under a name of its own choosing; the Act calls it "the ombudsman scheme" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=597]], s. 225(1)–(3)).
- **Compulsory jurisdiction.** A complaint must be dealt with under the scheme if three conditions are met. The complainant is eligible and wants the scheme. The respondent was an authorised person, an e-money issuer or a payment service provider at the time of the act or omission. The FCA's compulsory jurisdiction rules covered the activity at that time ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=597]], s. 226(1)–(3)). The FCA names those activities, and only regulated activities, or activities that could be made regulated, qualify ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=597]], s. 226(4)). The consumer credit jurisdiction in s. 226A was omitted from 1 April 2014 ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=599]], F1747).
- **Voluntary jurisdiction.** Firms that join the scheme can be covered for complaints the compulsory jurisdiction can't take, under rules the scheme operator makes with the FCA's approval ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=599]], s. 227(1)–(3); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=600]], s. 227(6)).
- **When it can act.** Only after the firm's final response, or after the firm's deadline has passed: eight weeks for most complaints, and 15 or 35 business days for payment-services and e-money complaints. It can act earlier only with the firm's consent ([[raw/fca-disp-2-8-referral-time-limits.md]], DISP 2.8.1R). See [[wiki/concepts/Ombudsman referral time limits]].
- **How complainants hear of it.** Every final response must enclose the FOS's standard explanatory leaflet, give its website address and tell the complainant they may now refer the complaint to it ([[raw/fca-disp-1-6-complaints-time-limits.md]], DISP 1.6.2R(1)). See [[wiki/concepts/Final response]].
- **When it can't.** It cannot take complaints referred more than six months after the final response. Nor can it take complaints referred more than six years after the event, or, if later, three years after awareness. The exceptions are exceptional circumstances or the firm's consent ([[raw/fca-disp-2-8-referral-time-limits.md]], DISP 2.8.2R).
- **Who can use it.** It deals only with complaints by or for an eligible complainant: consumers, micro-enterprises, small businesses, small charities and trusts, CBTL consumers and guarantors, whose complaint arises from a listed relationship with the firm ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]). The Ombudsman decides a business's eligibility on evidence such as audited accounts or VAT returns ([[raw/fca-disp-2-7-complainant-eligibility.md]]). See [[wiki/concepts/Eligible complainant]].
- **How it decides (emphasis).** Under the compulsory jurisdiction, it determines a complaint by what is, "in the opinion of the ombudsman, fair and reasonable in all the circumstances of the case" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=601]], s. 228(2)). Both parties get a signed written statement with reasons. The statement sets a date by which the complainant must say whether they accept or reject the determination ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=601]], s. 228(3)–(4)).
- **When the decision binds (emphasis).**
  - **Accepted:** "binding on the respondent and the complainant and final" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=601]], s. 228(5)).
  - **No answer by the date:** treated as rejected ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=601]], s. 228(6)).
  - **Late acceptance:** still counts if the complainant hasn't rejected and conditions in the scheme operator's rules are met. The firm's notice of a deemed rejection must explain this ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=601]], s. 228(6A), (7A)).
- **Published decisions.** The scheme operator must publish a report of every determination, unless the ombudsman thinks that inappropriate. A report may not identify the complainant without their consent ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=605]], s. 230A(1)–(3)).
- **Power to require information (emphasis).** By written notice, an ombudsman may require a party to provide information or produce documents it considers necessary to determine the complaint, within a reasonable period it specifies ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=606]], s. 231(1)–(3)). If a party fails without reasonable excuse, the High Court, or in Scotland the Court of Session, may deal with it, and with a company's directors or officers, as if in contempt ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=606]], s. 232(1); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=607]], s. 232(2)–(3)).
- **Telling the FCA.** The scheme operator must pass to the FCA information it thinks would or might help the FCA advance its operational objectives ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=607]], s. 232A(1)).
- **Funding.** The FCA may make rules requiring authorised persons, e-money issuers and payment service providers to pay towards setting up the scheme and running the compulsory jurisdiction ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=608]], s. 234(1)). Section 234A, on funding by consumer credit licensees, was omitted from 1 April 2014 ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=609]], F1775).
- **Successor firms.** A complaint can be dealt with against a firm that has taken on the original respondent's liability ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=609]], s. 234B).
- **What it can award.** Money up to a cap, which is £455,000 for acts on or after 1 April 2019 on complaints referred from 1 April 2026; interest; costs; and directions to the firm. Firms must comply promptly, and awards can be enforced in the courts ([[raw/fca-disp-3-7-ombudsman-awards.md]]). See [[wiki/concepts/Ombudsman awards]].
- **In its own words.** It asks consumers to complain to the business first, because most complaints are resolved there. Its service is free, and consumers don't need a lawyer or a claims management company. It prefers complaints in the complainant's own words, and warns that AI tools can add inaccurate "legal sounding arguments" that cause delay. Complaints are made through an online form ([[raw/financial-ombudsman-how-to-complain.md]]).
