# Prudential Regulation Authority

The PRA is the Bank of England acting through its Prudential Regulation Committee. It regulates PRA-authorised persons for safety and soundness ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 2A, 2B(2)).

## What the sources say
- **Identity.** The PRA "is the Bank of England". It exercises its functions only through the Prudential Regulation Committee under Part 3A of the Bank of England Act 1998 ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2A(1)–(2)).
- **Population.** Its population is PRA-authorised persons: authorised persons whose permission includes at least one PRA-regulated activity under s. 22A ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2B(5)). It must maintain arrangements for supervising them ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2K). See [[wiki/concepts/Solo and dual regulation]].
- **Size of that population.** The PRA regulates and supervises "around 1,292 banks, building societies, credit unions, insurers and major investment firms", by its own count as of 2026-09-21 ([[raw/bank-of-england-prudential-regulation.md]]). See "Where sources disagree" for the FCA's earlier figure.
- **What its rules and supervision are for.** Its rules, in the PRA Rulebook, require firms to keep sufficient capital and adequate risk controls; it supervises so that it can step in if a firm is not run safely and soundly ([[raw/bank-of-england-prudential-regulation.md]]).
- **General objective.** Its general objective is promoting the safety and soundness of PRA-authorised persons. It pursues it mainly in three ways ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2B(2)–(3)):
  - making sure their business avoids any adverse effect on UK financial stability
  - minimising the effect of a failure
  - for ring-fenced bodies, protecting the continuity of core services in the UK
- **Insurance objective.** Protecting policyholders applies where insurance is PRA-regulated ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2C). The Treasury can add further objectives when it brings new activities into the PRA's scope ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2D).
- **Not a zero-failure regime.** Nothing in ss. 2B–2D requires the PRA "to ensure that no PRA-authorised person fails" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2G). Failure includes insolvency, a Banking Act 2009 stabilisation option, or the compensation scheme treating the firm as unable to pay claims ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2J(3)).
- **Secondary objectives.** Since 2023 these are competition (facilitating effective competition in markets served by PRA-authorised persons) and competitiveness and growth ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2H(1)–(1B)).
- **Strategy.** It must set a strategy after consulting the Bank's court of directors, review it every 12 months, and publish it ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2E).
- **Guidance on objectives.** It must publish guidance on how it advances its objectives for different categories of firm, after consulting the FCA ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2I).
- **Panels.** Its statutory panels are the PRA Practitioner Panel and the Insurance Practitioner Panel. It has no consumer panel of its own ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 2L–2MA). The FCA's Consumer Panel may send views to the PRA ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1Q(5A)).
- **Powers over the FCA.** It can direct the FCA not to use a power where that use may threaten financial stability, cause a damaging failure of a PRA-authorised person, or threaten core services ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3I). See [[wiki/concepts/FCA-PRA coordination]].
- **No review section.** Its own review sections (ss. 2O–2P) were omitted in 2017 ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], notes F83–F84).
- **Formed April 2013** as the UK's prudential regulator of deposit-takers, insurers and major investment firms, after the global financial crisis ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=5]]).
- **Its own account of its objectives** matches the Act: two primary objectives (safety and soundness; policyholder protection for insurers) and two secondary ones (competition; competitiveness and growth) ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=8]]). It reads the competition objective as a duty to consider, not to adopt, options that favour competition ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=9]]).
- **How it supervises banks.** Judgement-based, forward-looking and focused on key risks, applied proportionately ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=5]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=14]]). Each firm has a potential impact category and a PIF stage; see [[wiki/concepts/Potential impact categories]] and [[wiki/concepts/Proactive Intervention Framework]]. Each firm's supervisory strategy is set at a Periodic Summary Meeting and sent to it by letter ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=47]]).
- **Leads bank authorisation.** It leads a single administrative process for deposit-takers and is the decision maker, with the FCA assessing conduct ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=42]]). See [[wiki/concepts/New bank authorisation]].
- **Giving and changing permissions, in the Act.** The PRA is the appropriate regulator where the activities include a PRA-regulated activity, and gives a [[wiki/concepts/Part 4A permission]] only with the FCA's consent ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=83]], s. 55A(2)(a); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=86]], s. 55F(2)). On a PRA-authorised firm's application it varies the permission with the FCA's consent, and cancels it after consulting the FCA; it may refuse to advance any of its objectives ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=89]], s. 55I(1)–(2); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=90]], s. 55I(4)). On its own initiative it may vary, cancel or impose requirements where a PRA-authorised firm is failing or likely to fail its threshold conditions, has not used a permitted activity for 12 months, or where that advances any of its objectives; it must consult the FCA, and needs its consent to add an activity or widen a description ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=90]], s. 55J(1); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=91]], s. 55J(3), (5); [[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-2026-09.pdf#page=98]], s. 55M(2)–(3)).
- **Powers.** Information gathering, s. 166 skilled persons reports, [[wiki/concepts/Part 4A permission|Part 4A]] powers to vary permissions or impose requirements, ring-fenced group restructuring, and enforcement (penalties, censure, prohibition) ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=50]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=51]]).
- **Not a fraud regulator.** Its inspections are not designed to uncover all malpractice ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=49]]).
- **Decisions.** Its most important decisions are made by the [[wiki/entities/Prudential Regulation Committee]] ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=48]]). The [[wiki/entities/Financial Policy Committee]] can make recommendations and give directions to it ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=15]]).

- **Lead regulator for dual-regulated firms.** Under the 2024 MoU it leads the single administrative process for dual-regulated firms: permissions (with FCA consent), PRA-designated senior managers, change in control and Part VII transfers of banking and ring-fencing schemes ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=6]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=21]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=22]], [[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=24]]). See [[wiki/concepts/FCA-PRA coordination]].
- **Funding.** Funded by fees on the regulated community; the FCA may collect them on its behalf ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=17]]).
