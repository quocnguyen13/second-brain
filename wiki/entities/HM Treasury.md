# HM Treasury

In FSMA 2000 Part 1A, "the Treasury" holds the statutory levers over the FCA and the PRA: orders, directions, regulations and recommendations on "the economic policy of Her Majesty's Government" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 1JA, 3G, 3RC, 3RE).

## What the sources say
- **Scope and objectives.** It can amend parts of the FCA's competition objective, the definition of "consumer", and the definition of "regulated financial services" ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1J). It can make activities PRA-regulated and give the PRA an extra objective for them ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2D).
- **The FCA–PRA boundary.**
  - It can specify by order which matters are, or are mainly, one regulator's responsibility for PRA-authorised persons ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3G).
  - Such an order needs approval by both Houses of Parliament. In an urgent case it can be made first, and lapses after 28 days unless both Houses approve it ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3H).
- **Recommendations.** It must send the FCA recommendations on the government's economic policy at least once each Parliament. It publishes them and the FCA's responses, and lays them before Parliament ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1JA).
- **Reviews.** It can appoint an independent person to review the FCA's use of resources ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 1S). It can direct a regulator to review specified rules that have been in force for at least 12 months ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3RC). It can require rules to be made on a specified activity, but not their content or outcome ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3RE).
- **Panels.** Its approval is needed to appoint or dismiss the chair of each statutory panel ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], ss. 1N(3), 1O(4), 1P(3), 1Q(3), 2M(3)).
- **The FCA's account.** The FCA describes itself as accountable to the Treasury, "which is responsible for the UK's financial system", and to Parliament ([[raw/fca-about-the-fca.md]]).
- **Parliamentary scrutiny of the FCA.** In 2016 the Commons Library described the FCA's accountability to the Treasury and Parliament as working "notably through regular appearances before the Treasury Select Committee" ([[raw/edmonds-financial-conduct-authority-2016.pdf#page=2]] · secondary).
- **Regulatory failure.** The Financial Services Act 2012 requires the PRA to investigate and report to the Treasury on events that indicate possible regulatory failure; firm failures don't automatically indicate one ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=13]]).
- **Macroprudential tools.** The tools the FPC can direct the PRA to use are specified by the Treasury in secondary legislation ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=15]]).
- **Designated activities.** Under FSMA Part 5A it may designate activities connected with UK financial markets or products; the FCA may then make rules on them if the Treasury's regulations allow ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=5]]). It can also require the regulators to review specified rules ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=5]]).
- **Assimilated law.** It commences the revocation of assimilated (formerly retained EU) law under FSMA 2023 Part 1, so the regulators can replace it in their rulebooks ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=25]]).
- **Coordination.** It receives the FCA–PRA memorandum of understanding and lays it before Parliament ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3E(5)–(6)). It also receives copies of PRA directions to the FCA under s. 3I ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3L(5)).
