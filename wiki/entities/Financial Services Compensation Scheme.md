# Financial Services Compensation Scheme

The UK's compensation fund of last resort for customers of authorised financial services firms ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=16]]).

## What the sources say
- **Depositors.** It may pay compensation for covered deposits if a firm is, or is likely to be, unable to pay claims. A PRA–FSCS MoU sets out how the two co-operate ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=16]]).
- **In resolution.** For smaller deposit-takers, failure usually means insolvency with covered depositors paid by the FSCS, within a target of seven days for most customers, using each bank's Single Customer View ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=37]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=38]]).
- **In the PIF.** The FSCS may be asked to check SCV data at stage 2 and may pay out or fund a transfer at stage 5 ([[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=45]], [[raw/pra-approach-banking-supervision-2023.pdf#page=47]]).
- **Who makes its rules.** Secondary legislation under FSMA gives the PRA the rules for the depositor and policyholder protection schemes, and the FCA the rules for schemes covering other regulated activities. The two co-ordinate on FSCS board appointments and consult each other before making FSCS rules ([[raw/fca-boe-memorandum-of-understanding-2024.pdf#page=11]]).
- **In statute.** A firm is treated as failing if the compensation scheme takes the view it is unable to pay claims ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 2J(3)). The FCA–PRA memorandum must cover relations with the compensation scheme ([[raw/uk-parliament-fsma-2000-part-1a.md]], s. 3E(3)).
