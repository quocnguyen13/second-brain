# Compare the PARA method and evergreen notes as ways to organise what I read

**Question:** Compare the PARA method and evergreen notes as ways to organise what I read

## Answer
The two methods answer different needs, and the sources disagree on how notes should be structured. PARA files each item in one known home inside four top-level folders and organises by the projects and goals you are committed to now. Reading material fits its Resources category, "topics you're interested in and learning about". Evergreen notes turn what you read into atomic, concept-oriented, densely linked notes. They accumulate across projects and use associative links instead of a hierarchy. PARA is built for finding material fast for active work. Evergreen notes are built for "better thinking", and Matuschak names writing about what you read as one of the practices. So for the reading itself, evergreen notes fit the stated goal more closely, and PARA fits the working files of live projects.

## Evidence
- PARA uses four top-level folders (Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives), "each containing a small number of subfolders" ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]), via [[wiki/concepts/PARA method]]
- Each item has one home: "you'll know exactly where to put everything, and exactly where to find it" ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]), via [[wiki/concepts/PARA method]]
- Organise "according to the projects and goals you are committed to right now", not broad subjects. In a busy day there is no time for "rummaging through a vast category like 'Psychology'" ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]), via [[wiki/concepts/Organizing by actionability]]
- Resources are "topics you're interested in and learning about" ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]), via [[wiki/concepts/PARA method]]
- Evergreen notes are "written and organized to evolve, contribute, and accumulate over time, across projects". The aim is "better thinking", not "better note-taking" ([[raw/matuschak-evergreen-notes.md]]), via [[wiki/concepts/Evergreen notes]]
- Five principles: atomic, concept-oriented, densely linked, associative ontologies over hierarchical taxonomies, written for yourself by default ([[raw/matuschak-evergreen-notes.md]]), via [[wiki/concepts/Evergreen notes]]
- Named reading practices: "Write about what you read to internalize texts deeply" and "A reading inbox to capture possibly-useful references" ([[raw/matuschak-evergreen-notes.md]]), via [[wiki/concepts/Evergreen notes]]

## Where sources disagree
- **A hierarchy with one home per item, or associative links.**
  - Forte: four top-level folders with subfolders, organised by project, so each item has one known home ([[raw/forte-para-method.md]]).
  - Matuschak: "Prefer associative ontologies to hierarchical taxonomies". Notes are concept-oriented and outlive any one project ([[raw/matuschak-evergreen-notes.md]]).
  - Bush agrees with Matuschak. Filing an item in "only one place" under one classification path is artificial, because "the human mind does not work that way. It operates by association" ([[raw/bush-as-we-may-think.pdf#page=14]]).
  - Scope: Forte files working material for action; Matuschak aims at "better thinking". They may suit different layers more than they compete.

## Caveats and gaps
- The captured Matuschak page gives each principle and practice as a title only. His reasons, and how "Write about what you read" works, are in linked notes not yet ingested. Ingesting that note would settle how he handles reading.
- (general knowledge) Many practitioners combine the two, using PARA folders for working files and a linked-notes area for ideas. Forte's later "Building a Second Brain" material describes distilling notes from what you read. Neither is in the wiki.
- (general knowledge) The trade-off in effort: PARA costs little per item (just file it), while evergreen notes cost more (rewrite and link each one).
